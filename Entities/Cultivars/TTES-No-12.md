**Other recorded attributes:**
- **Processing suitability:** listed under the tea-type codes above; Lee et al. 2017 flag it "f", a "popular or distinguishing tea variety in Taiwan".
- **Acreage:** second most widely planted cultivar in Taiwan at **13.7%** of tea acreage, behind Chin-Shin-Oolong (57.3%) and ahead of Shy-Jih-Chuen (13.2%). The top three cover over 84.2% (Hu et al. 2014, citing 2011 TRES estimates). It is one of the top five cultivars (with Chin-Shin-Oolong, Shy-Jih-Chuen, Chin-Shin-Dahpan and TTES-13), which Hu et al. 2014 describe as the cultivars suited to both Paochong and Oolong tea. It is also among the 12 dominant cultivars selected for that study, which together take over 98% of Taiwan's tea acreage (Hu et al. 2014, 2011 TRES statistics).
- **Lee et al. 2017** count it among the 25 "important and distinctive varieties" with a wide cultivation area. It is also one of four varieties (with Chin Shin Oolong, Shy Jih Chuen, TTES No.13 Tzuiyu) "currently produced and sold in bulk in Taiwan".
- **Morphology vs. TTES No. 13:** per Lai et al. 2001, "TTES No. 12" and "TTES No. 13" are difficult to tell apart morphologically but differ in parentage, cultivation method and tea quality. Both RAPD and ISSR profiles separate them.

## Parentage Notes

<!-- Describe the breeding cross in prose. Note if sources disagree on parentage. -->

Per Hu et al. 2014 (Table 1), TTES No. 12 comes from the cross **Tainon-8 (TW) × Ying-Jy-Horng-Shin (TW)**. The table footnote explains the intermediate line: **Tainon-8 = Hwang-Gan × Chin-Shin-Oolong**. The full pedigree is therefore (Hwang-Gan × Chin-Shin-Oolong) × Ying-Jy-Horng-Shin, which involves three Taiwanese landraces. Hu et al. 2014 classify all three as *C. sinensis* var. *sinensis* (L7, L1 and L5 in Table 1).

- Hu et al. 2014 write the cross as "A × B" but never say which parent is the seed (female) parent and which is the pollen parent. Parent-1/parent-2 above follow the order printed in the table and do not encode cross direction.
- Hu et al. 2014 say that Chin-Shin-Oolong, Dah-Yeh-Oolong, Chin-Shin-Dahpan, Ying-Jy-Horng-Shin, Hwang-Gan and Horng-Shin-Dahpan "have been used as female parents for hybridization breeding" since 1945. They do not apply this to TTES-12 specifically.
- **Classification labels vs. the documented pedigree.** Hu et al. 2014 (type S) and Hu et al. 2023 (group Sinensis) both fit the Hu 2014 pedigree, which contains only var. *sinensis* landraces. Hu et al. 2023's origin label "Hybrid" means bred from a cross, which also fits. Lai et al. 2001's "hybrid clones between China and Assam tea" does not fit the Hu et al. 2014 pedigree; see Source Conflicts.
- Hu et al. 2014 is the only vault source that gives a pedigree. Lai et al. 2001 say only that TTES No. 12 and No. 13 differ "in parentage", without naming the parents. Lee et al. 2017 and Hu et al. 2023 give no pedigree.
- **Molecular placement consistent with pedigree:** in Hu et al. 2014's UPGMA dendrogram (Fig. 3), TTES-12 falls in **Group IIIb**, described as "Tiee-Guan-In, Hwang-Gan, and its derived varieties (TTES-10, TTES-12, TTES-14, TTES-15, and TTES-19)". Its other named parent, Ying-Jy-Horng-Shin, sits in Group IIIc with its derived TTES-13.
- Tainon-8 also appears as a separate accession, "Tainon No.8" (H004, "Selected", Sinensis), in Hu et al. 2023. That paper does not connect it to TTES No. 12.

**Descendants:**
- **TTES No. 19** (TTES-19, H19) = **TTES-12 × Chin-Shin-Oolong**, released 2004 (Hu et al. 2014, Table 1 and Discussion citing Tsai et al. 2004a). Hu et al. 2014 name Chin-Shin-Oolong as the *male* parent of TTES-19, which implies TTES-12 was the seed parent of TTES-19.

**"TTES No.12 Zi-Ya No.1" (L056) is a separate accession.** Hu et al. 2023 (Table 1) list "TTES No.12 Zi-Ya No.1" as accession **L056**: group Sinensis, origin **"Selected"**, sampled at the Wenshan plantation. It is a different row with a different code from **V012** "TTES No.12", whose origin is "Hybrid". It sits in the "Selected" block next to Zi-Ya-Cha No.113 (L054) and Zi-Ya-Cha No.117 (L055), both Assamica selections. Hu et al. 2023 report that in all 10,296 pairwise comparisons of their 144 accessions, every cultivar differed from every other at ≥1 of the 12 SSR loci. So L056 and V012 have different genotypes and are not the same clone. The paper does not explain what "Zi-Ya" means or how L056 relates to V012 (e.g. seedling selection, sport, or name only). This note does not treat L056 as part of TTES No. 12, and any relationship between them is unresolved (see Open Questions).

## Geographic Range

<!-- Which named regions grow this cultivar? Link to Region notes. -->

**National distribution:** Hu et al. 2014 say that of the top five cultivars, Shy-Jih-Chuen is grown mainly in Nantou County and Chin-Shin-Dahpan mainly in the north-west, "while others [including TTES-12] are distributed around Taiwan" (2011 TRES estimates).

**Domestic made-tea samples labelled TTES No.12 (Chinhsuan), Lee et al. 2017 (Table 2; collected Nov 2011 via the TRES Tungding Substation):**
- [[Pinglin]], New Taipei City (PJK570, 574)
- Jhushan, Nantou County (PJK0075–0077)
- Mingjian, Nantou County (PJK130–131, 143)
- [[Alishan]], Chiayi County (PJK1136)
- Taitung County (PJK448, 450–451)
- Yilan County (PJK907–909)
- Hualien County (PJK1034, 1036, 1038)

These are the production sites of the tea samples tested. They show TTES No. 12 being produced at these sites, not its share of local acreage.

**Processed specialty teas found to contain V012, Hu et al. 2023 (Table 2, Fig. 4):**
- ST04 Tongding Oolong, Lugu Farmers' Association ([[Lugu]]), Spring 2020: a mixture. Single granules were assigned to either L001 (Chin-Shin-Oolong) or V012.
- ST05 Tieh-Kuan-Yin tea, Mucha Farmers' Association, Spring 2020: a mixture of V012 and I031 (Tie-Guan-Yin), with V012 dominant in the multi-granular samples.
- ST06 Black Oolong tea, private manufacturer in [[Luye]], Taitung, Spring 2021: 8 of 10 single granules "unequivocally" assigned to V012, the other two undetermined.

**Reference/germplasm plantings:**
- TRES germplasm garden, Taoyuan County (Hu et al. 2014 sample source)
- TRES Wenshan experimental plantation (V012 sampling site, Hu et al. 2023)
- Taitung Substation of TRES (Lee et al. 2017 reference plant)
- Yuchih Substation of the Taiwan Tea Experiment Station (Lai et al. 2001 sample source)

**Outside Taiwan:**
- Hu et al. 2023 say TTES No. 12 has been "cultivated in other countries for lower growing and production costs". They say the processed products are imported into Taiwan, reprocessed and labelled as counterfeit Taiwan specialty tea.
- Lee et al. 2017 found that 10 of 30 imported made teas were similar to TTES No. 12 by ITS2. Of the 30, 19 matched Taiwan cultivars, "especially those imported from Vietnam and Indonesia". Imported samples labelled TTES No.12 (Chinhsuan) in their Table 2 come from Bao Loc and Da Lat (Lam Dong, S. Vietnam), Moc Chau (Son La, N. Vietnam), "South Vietnam" and Sumatra (Indonesia). Lee et al. suggest the Vietnam/Fujian/Guangdong similarities may come from "the flow-out of Taiwanese tea plants; in particular, TTES12 and TTES13". They suspect the plants left Taiwan "through unofficial channels".

## Relationship to Wild Species

<!-- Does this cultivar trace back to C. formosensis? Note the lineage path. -->

No vault source places *C. formosensis* (Taiwanese wild tea) in the lineage of TTES No. 12. The only pedigree given (Hu et al. 2014) is made up entirely of *C. sinensis* var. *sinensis* landraces: Hwang-Gan, Chin-Shin-Oolong and Ying-Jy-Horng-Shin. In Hu et al. 2014's analysis, TTES-12 clusters within the sinensis group (Group III), apart from the *C. formosensis* group (Group I). Lee et al. 2017 say in general terms that TRES varieties were bred by crossing and selection that drew on native Taiwanese wild tea, but they do not say this for TTES No. 12.

## Molecular Identification

<!-- Added for this cultivar: marker/barcode evidence relevant to identifying TTES No. 12. -->

- **Hu et al. 2014 (STS/CAPS):** TTES-12 is one of the 12 prevailing cultivars identified with the five core markers (M02, C02, G01, G03, G04). M02 separates sinensis from assamica types, and G03/G01/G04 separate the eight sinensis-type cultivars, TTES-12 among them. It was also one of the eight cultivars used to pre-screen primers.
- **Hu et al. 2023 (SSR, TTVID kit):** V012 was one of six cultivars "commonly grown in Taiwan" used to screen the 83 candidate SSR primer pairs. Its amplicons, together with Chin-Shin-Oolong's (L001), were run as **reference samples in every capillary electrophoresis run**. It was detected in three of nine processed specialty teas (see Geographic Range).
- **Lee et al. 2017 (ITS2 barcode):** used the TTES No.12 (Chinhsuan) ITS2 sequence as the worked example of the BEL-1/BEL-3 amplicon (Fig. 2). Main findings:
  - 11 made teas clustered as TTES No.12 Chinhsuan at the ~0.009 genetic-distance cut-off (clusters 1 and 5, Fig. 3).
  - Across 18 made-tea samples from seven Taiwan production areas plus the TRES reference plant (19 total), ITS2 similarity ranged 0.910–1.000. Every made-tea sequence differed from the TRES reference plant.
  - SNP frequency (of 160 sites) was lowest in Jhushan at 1.3% (2/160) and highest in Pinglin at 9.4% (15/160). The domestic Chinhsuan average was 5.1% (8.1/160) and the imported Chinhsuan average 11.6% (18.5/160).
  - The three Hualien samples were identical to each other.
  - Chinhsuan showed 32 SNP sites in total (20.0%).
  - The TRES Chinhsuan reference carries c.112G>A in the per-variety alignment. In the all-sample alignment (Table 5, "All") it is listed as c.101G>A, and Lee et al. note that SNP positions shift when more samples are aligned.

## Source Conflicts

<!-- Explicitly document any disagreement between sources on any field above. -->
<!-- Format: [Source A] says X. [Source B] says Y. Decision: pending/use A/use B. Reason: -->

- **China × Assam hybrid ancestry.** [[laiAssessmentGeneticRelationships]] (Lai et al. 2001) lists TTES No. 12 as "Hybrid tea 2", one of its 7 "hybrid clones between China and Assam tea". [[huDevelopmentSTSCAPS2014]] (Hu et al. 2014) gives the cross as Tainon-8 (Hwang-Gan × Chin-Shin-Oolong) × Ying-Jy-Horng-Shin, all var. *sinensis* landraces (L7, L1, L5 in Table 1), and codes TTES-12 **S** rather than **SA** (var. *sinensis* × var. *assamica* hybrid). Decision: use [[huDevelopmentSTSCAPS2014]] (2014). Reason: Newest-source rule. Caveat: Lai et al. 2001 gives no pedigree or rationale for the grouping, and Hu et al. 2014 does not comment on it. The same discrepancy affects TTES No. 13 in Lai et al. 2001, so it may reflect how Lai et al. grouped all TRES-bred clones rather than a specific claim about TTES No. 12.

Hu et al. 2023's genetic group and origin label and Lee et al. 2017's species code are classification-scheme values, kept as separate rows in Known Facts and not treated as conflicts.

## Open Questions

- **Chinese name:** no vault source gives a Chinese name (臺茶12號 / 金萱 or similar). A TBRS cultivar page or registration document is needed before `name-zh` can be filled.
- **Year released/named:** no vault source gives it. Hu et al. 2023 say only "before 2004".
- **Breeding code / selection number:** none in the vault sources. H12 and V012 are study accession codes.
- **English/romanised name:** "Chinhsuan" comes from one source (Lee et al. 2017). Other romanisations (e.g. "Jin Xuan") do not appear in any vault source.
- **Cross direction:** Hu et al. 2014 don't say whether Tainon-8 or Ying-Jy-Horng-Shin was the seed parent. A second independent pedigree source (e.g. a TBRS breeding report) is needed to raise `parent-confidence`.
- **L056 "TTES No.12 Zi-Ya No.1":** what is it (seedling/sport selection from TTES No. 12, or unrelated), and what does "Zi-Ya" mean? Hu et al. 2023 give only the table row. Should it get its own entity note?
- **China × Assam hybrid ancestry:** the Lai 2001 vs. Hu 2014 disagreement is set to Hu et al. 2014 (all-sinensis pedigree) by the newest-source rule; a reviewer should confirm (see Source Conflicts).
- **Primary growing regions:** sources give only "distributed around Taiwan" plus the sites of tested samples. No source gives acreage by county or region.
- **Current acreage:** the 13.7% figure is from 2011 TRES estimates (via Hu et al. 2014). No more recent figure is in the vault.
- Hu et al. 2014 also write "Chih-Shih-Dahpan" for Chin-Shin-Dahpan in one passage, a spelling slip in a sibling cultivar name. It does not affect this note.
