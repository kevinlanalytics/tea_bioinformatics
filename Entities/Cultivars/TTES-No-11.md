**Names and codes found in sources:**
- "TTES No.11 (TTES-11)", study accession code **H11**, type "Developed variety", per Hu et al. 2014 (Table 1).
- "TTE No. 11", listed as "Hybrid tea 1", per Lai et al. 2001. Lai et al. write "TTES No. 12" and "TTES No. 13" for the neighbouring clones, so "TTE" appears to be a variant spelling of the same designation (identity assumed, not stated by the paper).
- "TTES No.11", accession code 11, per Lee et al. 2017 (Table 1). Lee et al. expand TTES as "Tea Research and Extension Substation, Executive Yuan, Taiwan ROC".
- "TTESNo.11", cultivar code **V011**, origin "Hybrid", per Hu et al. 2023 (Table 1).
- H11 and V011 are accession codes specific to those two studies, not breeding-line codes. No source gives a breeding code, a Chinese name (臺茶11號 / 台茶11號 do not appear in any source in the vault), or an English trade name.

**Other recorded traits:**
- Leaf type: **large** ("L"), per Lee et al. 2017 (Table 1, footnote d). Lee et al. give no fermentation-suitability code (NF/PF/F) for this accession.
- Morphologically "more similar to Assam tea" than to China tea, per Lai et al. 2001.
- Germplasm type: "Developed variety" (a bred variety from a cross), per Hu et al. 2014. Listed among "7 hybrid clones between China and Assam tea" developed in Taiwan "from hybridization and selection", per Lai et al. 2001.

## Parentage Notes

<!-- Describe the breeding cross in prose. Note if sources disagree on parentage. -->

Hu et al. (2014, Table 1) give the origin/parent of TTES-11 as **Dah-Yeh-Oolong (TW) × Jaipuri (IN)**. In the same table Dah-Yeh-Oolong is a var. *sinensis* landrace ("Planting in north and east of Taiwan"), and Jaipuri is an introduced var. *assamica* variety ("Original from India"). The same cross is listed for TTES-2. The species/variety column codes TTES-11 as **SA** (*C. sinensis* var. *sinensis* × var. *assamica* hybrid).

The table does not label which parent is the female. Elsewhere in its Discussion, Hu et al. (2014) say six landraces, Dah-Yeh-Oolong among them, "have been used as female parents for hybridization breeding" since 1945. That fits Dah-Yeh-Oolong as the seed parent and Jaipuri as the pollen parent, but the paper does not say so for TTES-11 specifically.

No other source in the vault gives parent names. Lai et al. (2001) describe the clone only as a China × Assam hybrid, which agrees at the variety level with a var. *sinensis* landrace × Indian Assam-type parent. Lee et al. (2017) and Hu et al. (2023) give no pedigree. Hu et al. (2023) record "Hybrid" as the origin, but that paper uses "Hybrid" for every TTES V-code entry, including TTES No. 24, which other sources describe as a wild-tea selection (see [[huSSRbasedMolecularDiagnosis2023]] conflicts). So the 2023 label does not independently confirm a cross.

**Morphology versus molecular placement (agreement across sources, not a conflict):**
- Lai et al. (2001) found that "TTE No. 11" clustered with the China tea cultivars in the RAPD dendrogram, and again with ISSR ("As was demonstrated with RAPDs"), "though it is morphologically more similar to Assam tea". It was the one hybrid clone where clustering did not match morphology. The authors conclude it "is closer to China tea in overall genetic relationship". They compare it with Wachira et al. (1995), whose putative China × Assam hybrids clustered against their morphology in the opposite direction.
- Hu et al. (2014) likewise place TTES-11 in the sinensis-type Group III of their UPGMA dendrogram, in subgroup **IIIc** with Dah-Yeh-Oolong and its other derived varieties (TTES-2, TTES-16, TTES-17, TTES-20). It is not in the assamica-type Group II with Jaipuri.
- Lee et al. (2017) record a large leaf type, which fits Lai et al.'s morphological observation.

## Geographic Range

No source in the vault names commercial growing regions for TTES No. 11. The only locations tied to it are germplasm holdings where samples were collected:

- The germplasm garden of the Tea Research and Extension Station, Taoyuan County, per Hu et al. 2014.
- The Tea Research and Extension Substation (Taitung Substation) collection, per Lee et al. 2017.
- The TRES experimental plantation at Wenshan (sampling plantation for V011), per Hu et al. 2023.

These are collection sites, not cultivation regions, so `regions:` is left empty.

## Relationship to Wild Species

<!-- Does this cultivar trace back to C. formosensis? Note the lineage path. -->

No source traces TTES No. 11 to *Camellia formosensis*. Its recorded parents, Dah-Yeh-Oolong and Jaipuri, are a *C. sinensis* var. *sinensis* landrace and a var. *assamica* introduction (Hu et al. 2014). In Hu et al.'s (2014) UPGMA analysis, TTES-11 falls in the cultivated sinensis-type Group III, not the *C. formosensis* Group I. Hu et al. (2023) classify V011 in the "Sinensis" group, not "Formosana".

A reviewer should decide whether a cultivar node records the pedigree-based label (SA) or a group assignment.
- **Purpose wording (likely not a real conflict).** [[huDevelopmentSTSCAPS2014]] says green and black tea (G,B). [[leeDNABarcodeIdentification2017]] says green tea and "red tea" (tea-type codes 1, 3). Decision: pending. Frontmatter `purpose` records "green, black" from Hu et al. 2014.
