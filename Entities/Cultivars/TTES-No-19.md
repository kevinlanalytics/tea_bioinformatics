## Parentage Notes

<!-- Describe the breeding cross in prose. Note if sources disagree on parentage. -->

Per Hu et al. 2014 (Table 1), TTES No. 19 is a developed variety from the cross **TTES-12 (TW) × Chin-Shin-Oolong (TW)**. Both parents are Taiwanese. The Discussion states that Chin-Shin-Oolong "is also the male parent of another two new varieties, TTES-19 and TTES-20, which were released in 2004 (Tsai et al. 2004a)". That makes [[TTES-No-12]] the implied female parent. The paper lists TTES-12 first in the cross but never calls it the female parent outright.

Deeper pedigree, assembled from the same table and its footnote (Hu et al. 2014): TTES-12 = Tainon-8 × Ying-Jy-Horng-Shin, and Tainon-8 = Hwang-Gan × Chin-Shin-Oolong. Chin-Shin-Oolong therefore appears on both sides of TTES No. 19's pedigree: as its direct male parent and as a grandparent of TTES-12 through Tainon-8. This is my inference from combining the table rows. Hu et al. 2014 does not comment on it directly.

In Hu et al. 2014's UPGMA dendrogram, TTES-19 falls in Group IIIb with Tiee-Guan-In, Hwang-Gan, TTES-10, TTES-12, TTES-14 and TTES-15. It sits with its female-side parent TTES-12 and not with its male parent Chin-Shin-Oolong, which is in Group IIIa. The paper lists TTES-19 among the "derived varieties" in Group IIIb. Its wording ("Tiee-Guan-In, Hwang-Gan, and its derived varieties") is ambiguous about which ancestor is meant.

No other source in the vault gives a parentage for TTES No. 19. Hu et al. 2023 gives no parents, only its own origin label "Hybrid" and genetic group "Sinensis", both consistent with a cross between two var. *sinensis* parents. Lee et al. 2017 gives no parentage.

`parent-1` is TTES-12 (linked to its own note) and `parent-2` is Chin-Shin-Oolong, as printed in Hu et al. 2014. The parent order follows the paper's cross notation; the female/male roles are explained above.

## Relationship to Wild Species

<!-- Does this cultivar trace back to C. formosensis? Note the lineage path. -->

Its recorded lineage does not trace back to *C. formosensis*. Per Hu et al. 2014 (Table 1 and footnote), every named ancestor is a Taiwanese *C. sinensis* var. *sinensis* landrace or a cultivar derived from one: TTES-12, Chin-Shin-Oolong, Tainon-8, Ying-Jy-Horng-Shin and Hwang-Gan. TTES-19 itself is classed "S" (var. *sinensis*). Hu et al. 2023 classes it in the "Sinensis" group, not "Formosana".

## Molecular Evidence

<!-- Not a template section; included because the sources' TTES No. 19 content is mainly molecular. -->

- **STS/CAPS markers (Hu et al. 2014):** TTES-19 was one of 8 cultivars used to pre-screen primers. Five core markers (M02, C02, G01, G03, G04) distinguish it among the 12 prevailing Taiwan cultivars. M02 places it in the sinensis-type group, and G03/G01/G04 separate the 8 sinensis-type cultivars.
- **ITS2 barcoding (Lee et al. 2017):** Among the 25 important Taiwan varieties, the lowest pairwise ITS2 similarity was 0.529, between Yabukita (No. 55) and TTES No. 19.
- **SSR / TTVID kit (Hu et al. 2023):** V019 is part of the 144-cultivar reference panel genotyped with the 12-locus multiplex SSR kit. Plant-variety-right protection of cultivars such as V019 is part of the paper's stated motivation for cultivar authentication. The V019-specific genotype was not extracted into the literature note.
