# Red Jade (TTES No. 18)

**Chinese name:** 臺茶18號 (紅玉)
**Canonical ID:** `cultivar_18`

Other names in sources: 紅玉 / "Red Jade" (TBRS page; Jhou et al. 2024), "Taiwan Tea No. 18" (Jhou et al. 2024), "TTES no. 18" (Su et al. 2009), "TTES-18", sample code H18 (Hu et al. 2014), sample code V018 (Hu et al. 2023), Table 1 entry no. 18 (Lee et al. 2017).

Additional traits, all per the TBRS cultivar page ([[TTESNo18]]):
- Bred by the Tea and Beverage Research Station (茶及飲料作物改良場育成).
- Upright arbor tree form (直立型喬木); large-leaf type; early-maturing (早生種).
- Mature leaves elliptical. Buds pale yellow-green, sparsely hairy, without white down (不具白毫).
- Strong growth vigor, medium-strong pest and disease resistance, strong drought tolerance.
- Made tea: slightly coarse leaf strips, black with a purple sheen; liquor bright golden-red; taste rich, mellow and fresh; aroma with light cinnamon and mint notes (淡淡肉桂及薄荷香氣).

Corroborating or additional claims from other sources:
- Jhou et al. (2024) describe it as "renowned for its ease of cultivation and distinct mint and cinnamon undertones" and "well received by the market", citing Shen et al. 2015, which is not in the vault.
- Su et al. (2009) say it "has become a popular tea item".
- Lee et al. (2017) code it as large-leaf (L) and flag it "f", a "popular or distinguishing tea variety in Taiwan".
- Hu et al. (2014) count TTES-18 among 12 prevailing cultivars that together cover more than 98% of Taiwan's tea acreage (TRES 2011 statistics). They name TTES-18, TTES-8 and TTES-7 as "the excellent cultivars for making black tea". Their five core STS/CAPS markers (M02, C02, G01, G03, G04) distinguish all 12 prevailing cultivars, TTES-18 included.

Molecular data points:
- In Lee et al. (2017), the highest ITS2 sequence similarity among the 25 important varieties was TTES No.18 vs. TTES No.8, at 0.984.
- Hu et al. (2023) include TTES No.18 as V018 in the 144-cultivar TTVID 12-SSR reference database. The sample came from the TRES Yuchi experimental plantation. The paper reports no TTES No.18-specific result.

## Parentage Notes

<!-- Describe the breeding cross in prose. Note if sources disagree on parentage. -->

Four sources describe the cross, and all four agree on which parent is which. The Burma/Myanmar large-leaf parent is the **maternal (female)** parent. The Taiwanese wild tea is the **paternal (pollen)** parent. Their wording differs:

- **TBRS cultivar page ([[TTESNo18]])** names both accessions. Its exact wording is 「母本緬甸大葉種B-729，父本臺灣野生山茶B-607」: maternal parent Myanmar large-leaf type B-729, paternal parent Taiwan wild mountain tea B-607. It is the only source in the vault that gives the paternal accession code **B-607**. It calls the paternal parent "臺灣野生山茶" (Taiwan wild mountain tea) and does not give a Latin binomial.
- **Jhou et al. 2024 ([[jhouInfluenceNaturalLight2024]])** says "Myanmar large leaf Burma (B-729) was used as the female parent to crossbreed with *C. formosensis*". It gives the maternal accession code B-729 but no paternal accession code. The paternal parent is framed at species level as *C. formosensis*.
- **Su et al. 2009 ([[suConfirmationCamelliaFormosensis]])** says the cultivar was "developed via hybridization between the Taiwanese wild tea as the paternal plant and a cultivar from Burma as the maternal plant". It gives no accession codes. The paper's subject is the "Taiwanese wild tea", which it formally names *Camellia formosensis*, so the paternal parent is *C. formosensis* in the paper's own framing. The Burma parent is called only "a cultivar from Burma", with no variety stated.
- **Hu et al. 2014 ([[huDevelopmentSTSCAPS2014]])**, Table 1, H18 gives "Burma (MM) × Taiwanese wild tea (TW)". There are no accession codes, and the paper does not say whether "×" is written female × male. Burma is listed first, which matches the other sources if the usual female × male order applies. In the same paper, "Taiwanese wild tea" (the W1–W8 accessions) is coded F, *C. formosensis*. A separate introduced accession, I2 "Burma", is coded A, var. *assamica*, origin Myanmar. The paper does not say whether I2 is the TTES-18 parent or B-729.

Species framing of the maternal parent: none of the four sources gives a Latin name for B-729 or "Burma" in the TTES No. 18 context. "Large-leaf" (大葉種) is the TBRS and Jhou descriptor. The var. *assamica* label for a "Burma" accession comes only from the Hu 2014 and Hu 2023 germplasm tables (Hu 2023: I006 "Burma", Assamica, Imported; I006F "Burma No.761", Assamica, Imported). Neither table links that accession to B-729.

Hu 2023 and Lee 2017 do not state the parentage.

Classification labels and the pedigree: the cultivar's own labels come from three different schemes (see Known Facts) and are recorded side by side, not as a conflict. They sit uneasily with the documented cross. Hu 2014 codes H18 "A" (*C. sinensis* var. *assamica*); its table has SA/AS categories for sinensis × assamica crosses and F for *C. formosensis*, but no formosensis-hybrid category. Hu 2023 places V018 in its "Assamica" group ("the cultivars belonging to *C. sinensis* var. *assamica*") with origin "Hybrid". Lee 2017 codes it "(I)" *Camellia sinensis*, the same code it gives the native Taiwanese wild teas. Meanwhile [[TTESNo18]], [[suConfirmationCamelliaFormosensis]], [[jhouInfluenceNaturalLight2024]] and Hu 2014's own parent column give a Taiwan wild tea / *C. formosensis* paternal parent. None of the papers says whether its label is a taxonomic claim or a grouping convention.

Frontmatter `parent-1` / `parent-2` hold the accession codes as TBRS prints them (B-729, B-607); neither accession has its own entity note in the vault.

## Geographic Range

<!-- Which named regions grow this cultivar? Link to Region notes. -->

- [[Yuchi]] (魚池), Nantou County: "one of the main cultivated varieties for black tea in the Yuchi area" (南投魚池地區紅茶主要栽培品種之一), per [[TTESNo18]].
- Nantou County, more broadly: Hu et al. 2014 say TTES-18 (with TTES-8 and TTES-7) is "mainly planted in Nantou County", citing a TRES 2011 estimate.
- The Hu et al. 2023 V018 reference sample came from the TRES Yuchi experimental plantation. That shows where the sample was taken, not where the cultivar is grown commercially.
- Myanmar/Burma is the origin of the maternal parent B-729, per [[TTESNo18]] and [[jhouInfluenceNaturalLight2024]]. It is not a stated growing region of the cultivar.

## Relationship to Wild Species

<!-- Does this cultivar trace back to C. formosensis? Note the lineage path. -->

Yes: it is a first-generation cross with [[Camellia-formosensis]] as the **paternal** parent.

Lineage path: [[Camellia-formosensis]] (Taiwan wild mountain tea accession B-607) ♂ × Myanmar large-leaf B-729 ♀ → TTES No. 18.
- The B-607 accession code is from [[TTESNo18]] only.
- The *C. formosensis* identification of the paternal parent is explicit in [[jhouInfluenceNaturalLight2024]]. It follows from [[suConfirmationCamelliaFormosensis]], which uses "Taiwanese wild tea" for the taxon it names *C. formosensis*.

This matches the existing species note, `Entities/Species/Camellia-formosensis.md`, which records the Su et al. 2009 statement that wild tea was the paternal parent and the Burma cultivar the maternal parent.

Jhou et al. 2024 present Red Jade as showing "that *C. formosensis* is an excellent candidate for tea tree breeding". The vault does not say which wild population or collection site B-607 came from (see Open Questions).

## Source Conflicts

<!-- Explicitly document any disagreement between sources on any field above. -->
<!-- Format: [Source A] says X. [Source B] says Y. Decision: pending/use A/use B. Reason: -->

- **What happened in 1999.** [[TTESNo18]] says the cultivar was officially named (正式命名) in ROC year 88 (1999). [[jhouInfluenceNaturalLight2024]] says "In 1999, Taiwan Tea No. 18 (Red Jade) was created through crossbreeding" and "In 1999, Myanmar large leaf Burma (B-729) was used as the female parent to crossbreed with C. formosensis". Read literally, that makes 1999 the year of the cross. Decision: use [[TTESNo18]] (2026). Reason: Newest-source rule (2026-11-10). Earlier caveat: a cross and an official naming are unlikely in the same year for a perennial tree crop, but no source in the vault gives the cross year, so the reading of Jhou cannot be settled here.
- **Parentage.** No disagreement on parent identity or cross direction across [[TTESNo18]], [[jhouInfluenceNaturalLight2024]], [[suConfirmationCamelliaFormosensis]] and [[huDevelopmentSTSCAPS2014]]. They differ only in detail: accession codes appear only in TBRS (both) and Jhou (B-729 only).

## Open Questions

<!-- What do you still need to verify before flipping verified: true? -->

- **Breeding code / selection line number:** no source in the vault gives one.
- **Year of the cross and of selection/testing:** only the 1999 naming year is documented. Jhou et al.'s wording needs checking against a primary breeding report.
- **Provenance of B-607:** which wild *C. formosensis* population (Nantou, South or East per Su et al. 2009) or which germplasm garden accession it came from is not stated in any source.
- **Identity of B-729:** how it relates to the "Burma" accessions in Hu et al. 2014 (I2) and Hu et al. 2023 (I006 "Burma", I006F "Burma No.761"), and whether it is formally var. *assamica*, is not stated.
- **Hu 2014 cross notation:** whether "×" is female × male; the paper does not say.
- **Molecular confirmation of parentage:** no source in the vault reports a marker-based check (e.g., maternal chloroplast haplotype matching B-729, or paternal *C. formosensis* alleles). Hu 2014 genotyped H18 and the wild teas, but its text does not discuss TTES-18's placement in the PCoA or dendrogram.
- **Growing regions beyond Yuchi/Nantou:** current acreage and any other areas are not documented. Hu 2014's figure is a 2011 estimate.
- **Shen et al. 2015,** cited by Jhou et al. for the market and flavor claims, is not in the vault.
