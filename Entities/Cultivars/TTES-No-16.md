## Parentage Notes

<!-- Describe the breeding cross in prose. Note if sources disagree on parentage. -->

Per Hu et al. 2014 (Table 1), TTES No.16 is a developed variety from the cross **Tainon-355\* (TW) × Tainon-1958 (TW)**. This is the same cross listed for its sibling TTES No.17 (H17). Both parents are Taiwanese breeding lines. Hu et al. 2014 do not state which parent is maternal. They write the crosses as "A × B", and for TTES-18 elsewhere in the paper that order is maternal × paternal, but they don't say this is a general convention.

The asterisk on "Tainon-355" points to the table footnote, which gives the ancestry of the Tainon lines. The footnote has no entry for Tainon-355. It does define a **Tainon-335**:
- Tainon-335: Dah-Yeh-Oolong × Kyang (Hu et al. 2014, Table 1 footnote)
- Tainon-1958: Tainon-20 × Bair-Mau-Hour (same footnote)
- Tainon-20: Hann-Koou line (same footnote)

If the footnote entry is the right one, the grandparents of TTES No.16 would be Dah-Yeh-Oolong, Kyang (an Indian assamica introduction), Tainon-20 (a Hann-Koou line) and Bair-Mau-Hour. That would fit Hu et al.'s "SA" (sinensis × assamica) classification. This inference is not stated in any source and depends on resolving 355 vs 335.

**Parent-1 field.** `parent-1` holds "Tainon-355" exactly as Hu et al. 2014 Table 1 prints it ("Tainon-355*"). The PDF's text layer was checked on 2026-11-10: the table prints "Tainon-355*" and the footnote the asterisk points to defines "Tainon-335", so the discrepancy is in the published paper itself, not a conversion error. Which number is correct is unresolved (see Source Conflicts).

**Classification labels.** The sources use different schemes, recorded separately in Known Facts. Hu et al. 2014 code TTES-16 "SA" and Lai et al. 2001 call "Taicha 16" a China x Assam hybrid clone, consistent with the documented Tainon × Tainon cross and the footnote's Kyang (assamica) ancestry. Hu et al. 2023 place TTES No.16 in their "Sinensis" genetic group while labelling its origin "Hybrid"; their scheme has only Sinensis, Assamica and Formosana groups, and they do not comment on how hybrid cultivars are assigned. Lee et al. 2017 give only species-level *Camellia sinensis*.

**Lai et al. 2001** say that clones "Taicha 14", "Taicha 15", "Taicha 16" and "Taicha 17" "were developed from 'Peimaohu'". "Taicha 16" did not cluster with "Peimaohu" in their RAPD or ISSR dendrograms, but it showed high similarity to it (RAPD 0.80, ISSR 0.77). This fits Hu et al.'s footnote, which puts Bair-Mau-Hour in the ancestry through Tainon-1958, if "Peimaohu" and "Bair-Mau-Hour" are the same landrace. No vault source states that equivalence (see Open Questions).

**Hu et al. 2014** (UPGMA dendrogram, Results) place TTES-16 in **Group IIIc**, among the "derived varieties" of Dah-Yeh-Oolong (with TTES-2, TTES-11, TTES-17 and TTES-20). Bair-Mau-Hour sits in Group IIIa. The Dah-Yeh-Oolong link matches the footnote's Tainon-335 = Dah-Yeh-Oolong × Kyang.

Hu et al. 2023 list Tainon No.335 (H006) and Tainon No.1958 (H008) as "Selected" sinensis accessions sampled at Yangmei. They do not link either one to TTES No.16.

## Geographic Range

<!-- Which named regions grow this cultivar? Link to Region notes. -->

No vault source names a commercial growing region for TTES No.16. The sources only give where their research samples came from:
- Hu et al. 2023: sampled at the Yangmei plantation of the Tea Research and Extension Station (TRES).
- Lee et al. 2017: one of 113 varieties housed at the TRES Taitung Substation.
- Lai et al. 2001: all samples, including "Taicha 16", collected from the Yuchih Substation of the Taiwan Tea Experiment Station.
- Hu et al. 2014: germplasm collected mainly from the TRES germplasm garden, Taoyuan County. The paper doesn't list a site for each individual sample.

These are germplasm-repository sites, not cultivation regions, so `regions:` is left empty.

## Relationship to Wild Species

<!-- Does this cultivar trace back to C. formosensis? Note the lineage path. -->

No source links TTES No.16 to *Camellia formosensis*. Hu et al. 2014 classify it as SA (sinensis × assamica), and the ancestry in their footnote includes no wild-tea parent. In their UPGMA tree it falls in Group III (sinensis type), not Group I (*C. formosensis*). Hu et al. 2023 place it in the "Sinensis" group, not "Formosana".

- **Year released / registered:** not stated in any vault source.
- **Breeding code** (pre-release selection number): not stated. "H16" (Hu 2014) and "V016" (Hu 2023) are sample codes from those papers, not breeding codes, so `breeding-code` is blank.
- **Tainon-355 vs Tainon-335:** needs an official TBRS/TRES cultivar record or registration document to settle.
- **Which parent is maternal:** not stated explicitly.
- **Name equivalences assumed here:** that Lai et al.'s "Taicha 16" is TTES No.16 (Lai uses "TTES No." for 12 and 13 but "Taicha" for 14–17), and that Lai's "Peimaohu" is Hu's "Bair-Mau-Hour". Both are romanization equivalences that no source states outright.

- **Sibling relationship to TTES No.17:** Hu et al. 2014 list the same cross for both. No source discusses whether they are full sibs from one cross population.
