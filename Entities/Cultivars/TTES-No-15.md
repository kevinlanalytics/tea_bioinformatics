## Parentage Notes

<!-- Describe the breeding cross in prose. Note if sources disagree on parentage. -->

- **Hu et al. 2014** (Table 1) list TTES No.15 (H15) as a developed variety from **Tainon-983 × Bair-Mau-Hour**, both of Taiwanese origin. The Table 1 footnote says **Tainon-983 = Hwang-Gan × Kyang**. Hwang-Gan is a Taiwanese landrace (L7), and Kyang is an introduced variety of Indian origin (I10, assamica type), per the same table. That gives the pedigree **(Hwang-Gan × Kyang) × Bair-Mau-Hour**. Hu et al. 2014 list the same cross for **TTES No.14** (H14), so the two are sister cultivars on paper.
- Hu et al. 2014 do not say which parent is the female and which is the male for this cross. The order above (Tainon-983 first) is the paper's order. The Discussion's statement that six landraces have served as female parents since 1945 does not include Bair-Mau-Hour or Tainon-983, so it does not settle the direction.
- **Lai et al. 2001** say that "Taicha 14", "Taicha 15", "Taicha 16", and "Taicha 17" "were developed from 'Peimaohu'", and that Taicha 15 clustered with Peimaohu in both the RAPD and ISSR dendrograms, "reflecting pedigree relationship". Lai et al. name no second parent and do not say whether Peimaohu is a direct parent or a more distant ancestor. They class Taicha 15 as a China × Assam hybrid clone bred in Taiwan. That fits the Kyang (Assam) contribution through Tainon-983 that Hu et al. 2014 report, although Lai et al. do not name Kyang.
- "Peimaohu" (Lai et al. 2001) and "Bair-Mau-Hour" (Hu et al. 2014, Hu et al. 2023; "Bair Mau Hour" in Lee et al. 2017) look like two romanizations of the same landrace name. No source states this equivalence, so it is not treated as established here (see Open Questions).
- Note on Lai et al.'s TTES No.16 claim: Hu et al. 2014 give TTES No.16 and No.17 the parentage Tainon-355 × Tainon-1958, with Tainon-1958 = Tainon-20 × Bair-Mau-Hour. That is consistent with Lai et al.'s statement that all four were "developed from" Peimaohu, but it covers sibling cultivars, not No.15 itself.

## Geographic Range

<!-- Which named regions grow this cultivar? Link to Region notes. -->

No source gives commercial growing regions for TTES No.15. The sources record only where the sampled plants were collected. These are germplasm or experimental plantings, not production regions, so `regions:` is left empty:
- Hu et al. 2014: germplasm garden of the Tea Research and Extension Station, Taoyuan County (all accessions except Shan-1 to Shan-4).
- Lai et al. 2001: Yuchih Substation of the Taiwan Tea Experiment Station (all 37 samples).
- Hu et al. 2023: sampling plantation "Yangmei" (TRES, Taoyuan) for V015.
- Lee et al. 2017: TRES-housed varieties (Table 1). None of the domestic made-tea samples in Table 2 is identified as TTES No.15.

TTES No.15 is not among the 12 prevailing Taiwan cultivars whose acreage Hu et al. 2014 tabulate. Lee et al. 2017 also do not flag it as a "popular or distinguishing" variety (footnote f).

## Relationship to Wild Species

<!-- Does this cultivar trace back to C. formosensis? Note the lineage path. -->

No *C. formosensis* ancestry is reported. The sourced pedigree (Hu et al. 2014) runs through Hwang-Gan (landrace, sinensis), Kyang (introduced from India, assamica), and Bair-Mau-Hour (landrace, sinensis). None of these is Taiwanese wild tea. Hu et al. 2014 place TTES-15 in the sinensis-type group (Group III), not in Group I (*C. formosensis*). Hu et al. 2023 likewise classify V015 as "Sinensis", not "Formosana".

## Molecular / Genetic Data

- **Hu et al. 2014 (STS/CAPS, modified Roger's distance):** TTES-14 and TTES-15 have the **smallest pairwise genetic distance (0.11)** among the 37 sinensis-type accessions (range 0.11–0.62). This fits their identical listed parentage. In the UPGMA dendrogram, TTES-15 falls in **Group IIIb** with Tiee-Guan-In, Hwang-Gan, and "its derived varieties" (TTES-10, TTES-12, TTES-14, TTES-15, TTES-19). Bair-Mau-Hour itself falls in Group IIIa.
- **Lai et al. 2001 (RAPD/ISSR, Dice/UPGMA):** "Taicha 15" clusters with "Peimaohu" in both the RAPD and ISSR dendrograms.
- **Lee et al. 2017 (ITS2 barcoding):** TTES No.15 is one of the 113 varieties used to build the phylogenetic tree (Table 1, code 15). The text found contains no result specific to TTES No.15.
- **Hu et al. 2023 (12-locus SSR, TTVID kit):** V015 is in the 144-cultivar reference database. Across all 144 cultivars, every pair differed at 1 or more of the 12 loci. No result specific to V015 is reported in the text found.

- **Secondary processing suitability.** [[huDevelopmentSTSCAPS2014]] says "O,G" (oolong, green tea). [[leeDNABarcodeIdentification2017]] says tea types "2,6" (Oolong Tea, White Tea). Decision: pending. Reason: both agree on oolong but disagree on the second type. Neither cites an official registration document.
- **Clustering and lineage signal.** [[laiAssessmentGeneticRelationships]] says Taicha 15 clusters with Peimaohu (RAPD and ISSR), which they read as reflecting pedigree. [[huDevelopmentSTSCAPS2014]] says TTES-15 falls in Group IIIb with Hwang-Gan/Tiee-Guan-In and is described as a Hwang-Gan-derived variety, while Bair-Mau-Hour sits in Group IIIa. Decision: pending. Reason: the two studies use different marker systems and sample sets. Both results are compatible with the pedigree (Hwang-Gan × Kyang) × Bair-Mau-Hour, but they stress different parental lineages.
- **Taxonomic group label.** [[huDevelopmentSTSCAPS2014]] says SA (*C. sinensis* var. *sinensis* × var. *assamica* hybrid), placed within the sinensis type. [[laiAssessmentGeneticRelationships]] says China × Assam hybrid clone. [[huSSRbasedMolecularDiagnosis2023]] says "Sinensis" (in a three-group Sinensis/Assamica/Formosana scheme). Decision: pending. Reason: probably a difference in classification scheme (Hu et al. 2023 has no hybrid category) rather than a factual disagreement, but neither paper states this.
- **Named progenitor.** [[laiAssessmentGeneticRelationships]] says "developed from 'Peimaohu'" (single progenitor named). [[huDevelopmentSTSCAPS2014]] says Tainon-983 × Bair-Mau-Hour. Decision: pending. Reason: these are not contradictory if Peimaohu = Bair-Mau-Hour, but that equivalence is unsourced, and Lai et al. omit the Tainon-983 side entirely.
