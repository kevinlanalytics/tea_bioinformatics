# TTES No. 20

**Chinese name:** not found in the vault's sources (see Open Questions)
**Canonical ID:** `cultivar_20`

Accession codes in the studies: **H20** in Hu et al. (2014), listed as "TTES No.20 (TTES-20)". **V020** in Hu et al. (2023), listed as "TTES No.20". Code **20** in Lee et al. (2017), listed as "TTES No.20". These are per-study sample codes, not breeding codes.

Other sourced attributes:
- **Taxonomic group:** *C. sinensis* var. *sinensis* (Hu 2014 code "S", a developed variety). Hu 2023 also places it in the "Sinensis" group with origin "Hybrid". Lee 2017 codes it only "I" (*Camellia sinensis*, variety not specified).
- **Leaf type:** small ("S") per Lee 2017.
- **Legal status:** Hu 2023 names V020 as one of four cultivars (with V019, V022 and V025) protected under the Plant Variety and Plant Seed Act, enacted in 2004. Hu 2014 says the same for TTES-19 and TTES-20.
- **Status in the industry:** Hu 2014 counts TTES-20 among 12 "prevailing" or dominant cultivars chosen for its identification key. The selection criteria were acreage, suitability for unique teas, and being a newly bred variety, and the 12 together cover over 98% of Taiwan's tea acreage (2011 TRES statistics). Lee 2017 lists TTES No.20 among 25 "important and distinctive varieties [that] have a wide cultivation area", specifically as one of the "new cultivars TTES Nos. 18, 19, 20", and flags it "Popular or distinguishing tea variety in Taiwan". Hu 2023 uses V020 as one of six cultivars "commonly grown in Taiwan" for its initial SSR primer screening.

## Parentage Notes

<!-- Describe the breeding cross in prose. Note if sources disagree on parentage. -->

Only Hu et al. (2014) gives a pedigree: **2022 × Chin-Shin-Oolong**. Its Table 1 marks "2022" with an asterisk, and the footnote expands it to **Dah-Yeh-Oolong × Tainon-20**, where **Tainon-20 is a Hann-Koou line**. That gives this three-generation path:

- TTES No. 20 = ("2022" = Dah-Yeh-Oolong × Tainon-20 [Hann-Koou line]) × Chin-Shin-Oolong

The Discussion of Hu 2014 says outright that "Chin-Shin-Oolong is also the male parent of another two new varieties, TTES-19 and TTES-20, which were released in 2004 (Tsai et al. 2004a)". The paper never calls the "2022" line the female parent. Treating it as parent-1 (female) comes from the table's "A × B" order, which matches the paper's other crosses, e.g. TTES-18 "Burma × Taiwanese wild tea".

In Hu et al. (2014)'s UPGMA dendrogram (Fig. 3), TTES-20 sits in **Group IIIc** as one of the "derived varieties" of **Dah-Yeh-Oolong**, along with TTES-2, -11, -16 and -17. It does not sit in Group IIIa with its stated male parent Chin-Shin-Oolong. This is consistent with Dah-Yeh-Oolong being a grandparent through the 2022 line. Note that this placement is a marker-similarity result, not independent pedigree evidence.

Hu et al. (2023) list a separate accession, **"Tainon No.2022" (H003)**, in the Sinensis group with origin "Selected", sampled at Yangmei. That paper does not connect it to TTES No. 20. That H003 is the "2022" parent of Hu 2014 is a plausible guess, but no source says so. Do not confuse **Tainon No.20 (H005)**, a breeding line that Hu 2014 calls the Hann-Koou-line grandparent "Tainon-20", with this cultivar.

## Geographic Range

<!-- Which named regions grow this cultivar? Link to Region notes. -->

No source names a growing region for TTES No. 20. The only locations tied to it are sampling sites, which are not cultivation regions:
- Hu et al. (2023): the V020 sample came from the TRES **Wenshan** experimental plantation.
- Hu et al. (2014): germplasm came mainly from the TRES germplasm garden in Taoyuan County. The paper does not list a per-accession location for H20.

Lee 2017 and Hu 2014 both describe it as widely cultivated or prevailing, but neither says where it is grown.

## Relationship to Wild Species

<!-- Does this cultivar trace back to C. formosensis? Note the lineage path. -->

No *C. formosensis* ancestry appears in the sources. All the named ancestors in Hu 2014 are sinensis-type Taiwanese landraces or Chinese-introduced lines: Dah-Yeh-Oolong, Chin-Shin-Oolong, and Tainon-20 (Hann-Koou line, with Hann-Koou listed as introduced from China). Hu 2014 classifies TTES-20 as *C. sinensis* var. *sinensis* ("S"). Hu 2023 places it in the "Sinensis" group, not "Formosana".

## Source Conflicts

<!-- Explicitly document any disagreement between sources on any field above. -->
<!-- Format: [Source A] says X. [Source B] says Y. Decision: pending/use A/use B. Reason: -->

- **Origin label of the "2022" parent line.** [[huDevelopmentSTSCAPS2014]] says the "2022" line is a cross: Dah-Yeh-Oolong × Tainon-20. [[huSSRbasedMolecularDiagnosis2023]] lists "Tainon No.2022" (H003) with origin "Selected". Decision: pending. Reason: this may not be a real contradiction. Hu 2023 also labels Tainon No.8 "Selected", and Hu 2014 gives Tainon-8 as a cross (Hwang-Gan × Chin-Shin-Oolong). So "Selected" looks like Hu 2023's category for TRES breeding lines, not a claim that the line has no cross behind it. Neither paper confirms that H003 and the Hu 2014 "2022" are the same accession.
- **Taxonomic resolution (minor, not a factual disagreement).** [[huDevelopmentSTSCAPS2014]] and [[huSSRbasedMolecularDiagnosis2023]] put TTES No. 20 in *C. sinensis* var. *sinensis*. [[leeDNABarcodeIdentification2017]] codes it only "I" (*C. sinensis*, variety unspecified), as it does for all TTES numbers it lists. Decision: pending. Reason: Lee's code is coarser, not contrary.

- **Growing regions.** No sourced region or acreage data. A TBRS or TRES cultivar page for TTES No. 20, like the vault's [[TTESNo18]] and [[TTESNo24]] pages, would help.
- **Released vs. registered vs. protected.** Hu 2014 says "released in 2004" and "protected... since 2004". The literature note paraphrases this as "registered 2004". The exact event and date are not distinguished.

The literature note itself does not currently mention TTES No. 20; these details come from the full text.
