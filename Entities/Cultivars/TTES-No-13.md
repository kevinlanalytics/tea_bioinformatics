**Other attributes from the sources:**
- **Type of germplasm:** "Developed variety" (Hu et al. 2014). Lee et al. 2017 counts it among the 20 TRES breeding varieties (TTES 1–20).
- **Leaf type:** small (S) (Lee et al. 2017, Table 1).
- **Suitability:** Lee et al. 2017 marks TTES No.13 with footnote "f", "popular or distinguishing tea variety in Taiwan". Lee et al. 2017 also names it as one of four varieties (with Chin Shin Oolong, TTES No.12 (Chinhsuan) and Shy Jih Chuen) "currently produced and sold in bulk in Taiwan".
- **Cultivation status:** Hu et al. 2014 lists TTES-13 among the 12 "prevailing"/dominant cultivars that together "take over 98% acreage of Taiwan" (TRES 2011 statistics). Within those 12, it is named with Chin-Shin-Oolong, TTES-12, Shy-Jih-Chuen and Chin-Shin-Dahpan as "the top five cultivars in Taiwan that has been found to be suitable for both Paochong tea and Oolong tea". No acreage share is given for TTES-13 itself.

## Parentage Notes

<!-- Describe the breeding cross in prose. Note if sources disagree on parentage. -->

Per Hu et al. 2014 (Table 1), TTES-13 comes from the cross **Ying-Jy-Horng-Shin (TW) × Tainon-80 (TW)**:
- **Ying-Jy-Horng-Shin** (accession L5) is a sinensis-type landrace "planting in New Taipei City, Taiwan", suited to oolong. Hu et al. 2014 names it as one of four landraces brought from China during the Japanese occupation (1896–1945). It is also one of six landraces "used as female parents for hybridization breeding since 1945". Lee et al. 2017 spells it "Ying Jy Horng Shin" (C. sinensis var. sinensis, partially fermented, small leaf).
- **Tainon-80** is a breeding line. The Table 1 footnote in Hu et al. 2014 describes it only as "Tainon-80: Hann-Koou line". Hann-Koou (accession I15) is an introduced sinensis-type variety, "Original from China". Tainon-80 itself was not sampled in Hu et al. 2014. The 144-accession table in Hu et al. 2023 lists Tainon No. 8, 20, 335, 983, 1958, 2022 and others, but not Tainon No. 80.
- **Cross direction:** Hu et al. 2014 lists Ying-Jy-Horng-Shin first. The paper's general statement that landraces served as female parents fits Ying-Jy-Horng-Shin being the maternal parent, but the paper never states the seed/pollen direction for TTES-13 specifically. The parent-1/parent-2 order above follows the table order only.
- **Related cross (context only):** Hu et al. 2014 gives TTES-12 as Tainon-8 × Ying-Jy-Horng-Shin, so TTES-12 and TTES-13 share Ying-Jy-Horng-Shin as a parent, listed in opposite positions. Lai et al. 2001 says TTES No. 12 and TTES No. 13 "are difficult to distinguish morphologically" but "are different in parentage, method of cultivation, and tea quality". It does not give either pedigree.
- **Molecular placement:** In the UPGMA dendrogram of Hu et al. 2014 (Fig. 3), TTES-13 falls in sinensis subgroup **IIIc** with "Ying-Jy-Horng-Shin [and] its derived variety (TTES-13)". This fits the stated maternal landrace.

Only Hu et al. 2014 gives the pedigree. Lee et al. 2017, Lai et al. 2001 and Hu et al. 2023 give none, so the parentage is uncorroborated.

- **Classification labels vs. pedigree:** Hu et al. 2014's S code and Hu et al. 2023's "Sinensis" group both sit consistently with a cross of two sinensis-type parents. Hu et al. 2023's origin label "Hybrid" is a separate column and does not by itself say which varieties were crossed. Lai et al. 2001's "hybrid clones between China and Assam tea" does not fit the Hu et al. 2014 pedigree; see Source Conflicts.

## Geographic Range

<!-- Which named regions grow this cultivar? Link to Region notes. -->

- **Taiwan, island-wide:** Hu et al. 2014 says Shy-Jih-Chuen and Chin-Shin-Dahpan are grown mainly in Nantou County and north-west Taiwan, "while others are distributed around Taiwan". TTES-13 is one of those "others". No source in the vault names a county, township or tea district where TTES-13 is grown, so `regions:` is left empty.
- **Reference/germplasm plantings (sampling sites, not growing regions):**
  - Hu et al. 2014 collected germplasm mainly from the TRES germplasm garden in Taoyuan County.
  - Hu et al. 2023 sampled V013 at the TRES experimental tea plantation in **Wenshan** (Table 1 and footnote b).
  - Lee et al. 2017 used a Tzuiyu reference sample "housed at the TRES". The paper's tea plant varieties were provided by the Taitung Substation of TRES.
- **Outside Taiwan:** Lee et al. 2017 matched two imported made teas to TTES No.13 by ITS2: **TJK82** and **TJK107**, both from **Bao Loc, Lam Dong (South Vietnam)** (Table 5). The authors take such matches, together with 10 imports matched to TTES No.12, as suspected evidence that Taiwanese varieties were moved abroad "through unofficial channels". They also suggest that DNA similarities among teas from Taiwan, Fujian, Guangdong and Vietnam "may be a result from the flow-out of Taiwanese tea plants; in particular, TTES12 and TTES13". This is the authors' interpretation, not a documented planting record. Lee et al. 2017 contains no domestic TTES No.13 made-tea samples, so it gives no Taiwanese production sites for the cultivar.

## Relationship to Wild Species

<!-- Does this cultivar trace back to C. formosensis? Note the lineage path. -->

No source in the vault traces TTES No. 13 to *C. formosensis*. Per Hu et al. 2014 both recorded parents are sinensis-type: Ying-Jy-Horng-Shin (landrace from China) and Tainon-80 (a Hann-Koou line, with Hann-Koou introduced from China). TTES-13 clusters in the sinensis group (Group III, subgroup IIIc), apart from the *C. formosensis* group (Group I) (Hu et al. 2014, Fig. 3). Hu et al. 2023 puts V013 in the Sinensis group, not Formosana.

## Molecular Evidence

<!-- Not a template section; added because three of four sources are marker studies. -->

- **RAPD / ISSR (Lai et al. 2001):** RAPD and ISSR profiles both separated TTES No. 13 from the morphologically similar TTES No. 12, "indicating that there is genetic difference between them".
- **STS / CAPS (Hu et al. 2014):** TTES-13 was one of eight cultivars used to pre-screen the EST-derived primer pairs. It is one of the 12 prevailing cultivars that the five core markers (M02, C02, G01, G03, G04) can tell apart. Its genotype at those markers and its path through the identification flowchart were not found in the extracted text (they are probably in a figure).
- **ITS2 barcoding (Lee et al. 2017):** The ITS2 fragment of the made teas was 160 bp. In the alignment of the TRES Tzuiyu reference with the two Vietnamese imports, Tzuiyu showed 14 SNP sites (8.8% of sites), against 32 (20.0%) for Chinhsuan and 53 (33.1%) for Chin Shin Oolong. Provenance-characteristic SNPs listed in Table 5 are TJK82: c.8C>T, c.9G>T, c.12C>T, c.102G>A, c.122C>G; and TJK107: c.1C>G, c.3T>G, c.35C>T, c.45T>C, c.86G>A, c.92T>C, c.101G>A, c.103G>T, c.140T>C.
- **SSR (Hu et al. 2023):** V013 is one of the 144 cultivars in the 12-SSR TTVID genotype database. The extracted text does not discuss V013 beyond its Table 1 entry.

## Source Conflicts

<!-- Explicitly document any disagreement between sources on any field above. -->
<!-- Format: [Source A] says X. [Source B] says Y. Decision: pending/use A/use B. Reason: -->

- **China × Assam hybrid ancestry:** [[laiAssessmentGeneticRelationships]] (Lai et al. 2001) lists TTES No. 13 as "Hybrid tea 3", one of its 7 "hybrid clones between China and Assam tea". [[huDevelopmentSTSCAPS2014]] (Hu et al. 2014) gives the cross as Ying-Jy-Horng-Shin × Tainon-80, both sinensis-type (Ying-Jy-Horng-Shin, S landrace; Tainon-80, a Hann-Koou line, with Hann-Koou coded S), and codes TTES-13 **S** rather than **SA** (var. *sinensis* × var. *assamica* hybrid). Decision: use [[huDevelopmentSTSCAPS2014]] (2014). Reason: Newest-source rule. Caveat: Lai et al. 2001 gives no pedigree or rationale for the grouping, and Hu et al. 2014 does not comment on it. The same discrepancy affects TTES No. 12 in Lai et al. 2001, so it may reflect how Lai et al. grouped all TRES-bred clones rather than a specific claim about TTES No. 13.
