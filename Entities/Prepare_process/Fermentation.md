# Fermentation

## Definition
<!-- What happens during this process? Cite the source. -->

"Fermentation" is the industry term for an oxidation step in tea manufacture. Hu et al. 2023 (Table 2, footnote e) say the term "fermentation level" is used when talking to the public, growers and manufacturers, but that "in scientific speaking, this is an oxidation process in which no enzyme or no microorganism was involved." Their main text writes "fermentation (oxidation to be exact)" [[huSSRbasedMolecularDiagnosis2023]].

Hu et al. 2014 describe tea products as "manufactured through the application of high temperature and the use of fermentation treatments at a panning step" [[huDevelopmentSTSCAPS2014]]; see [[Panning]].

No source in the vault describes how the step is carried out (time, temperature, handling of the leaf). The Chinese term isn't printed in any vault source, so `name-zh` is blank.

## Distinguishing features
<!-- What separates it from similar processes? -->

- **Degree defines the tea type.** Hu et al. 2023 grade nine Taiwan specialty teas by fermentation level (Table 2) [[huSSRbasedMolecularDiagnosis2023]]:

  | Level | Teas (sample ID) |
  |---|---|
  | None | Taiwan Green tea / Pi Luo Chun (ST01) |
  | Light | Wenshan Paochong (ST02) |
  | Light to medium | High-Mountain Oolong (ST03) |
  | Medium | Tongding Oolong (ST04), Tieh-Kuan-Yin (ST05) |
  | Heavy | Black Oolong (ST06), Oriental Beauty (ST07) |
  | Full | Taiwan Black tea, var. *sinensis* (ST08) and var. *assamica* (ST09) |

  Hu et al. 2023 also call green tea "lightly fermented and non-roasted" in their introduction, while Table 2 lists their green tea sample (ST01) as fermentation "None". The two are compatible if "light" there is loose wording, but the paper doesn't reconcile them.
- **Separate from roasting.** Hu et al. 2023 record fermentation and roasting as two independent levels for each tea; Oriental Beauty is heavily fermented but not roasted (Table 2). See [[Roasting]].
- **Cultivar suitability categories.** Lee et al. 2017 classify each cultivar's processing suitability as NF (nonfermented), PF (partially fermented) or F (fermented) (Table 1 footnote) [[leeDNABarcodeIdentification2017]].
- **Effect on DNA.** Hu et al. 2014 say fermentation and high-temperature treatments "could eventually lead to dramatic DNA degradation" [[huDevelopmentSTSCAPS2014]]. Hu et al. 2023 found low DNA yields from teas "manufactured with more fermentation (oxidation), such as Black Oolong and Taiwan Black teas", and suggest the slower CTAB extraction for teas made by a heavily fermented process [[huSSRbasedMolecularDiagnosis2023]].

## Variations
<!-- Relevant differences in temperature, duration, or method, with sources. -->

- **By level:** none, light, light-to-medium, medium, heavy and full, as in the table above (Hu et al. 2023, Table 2).
- **By cultivar suitability:** NF / PF / F (Lee et al. 2017, Table 1).
- No vault source gives temperatures, durations or methods for any level.
