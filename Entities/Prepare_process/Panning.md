# Panning

## Definition
<!-- What happens during this process? Cite the source. -->

Panning is a high-temperature step in tea manufacture. Hu et al. 2014 write that tea products "are manufactured through the application of high temperature and the use of fermentation treatments at a panning step", and that "these processes could eventually lead to dramatic DNA degradation" [[huDevelopmentSTSCAPS2014]].

Lee et al. 2017 prepared their reference samples by a related sequence: the leaves "were fixed, dried, and processed without fermentation into green tea samples" [[leeDNABarcodeIdentification2017]]. They don't say how fixing was done or whether it was by panning.

Neither source describes the equipment, temperature or duration. The Chinese term isn't printed in any vault source, so `name-zh` is blank.

## Distinguishing features
<!-- What separates it from similar processes? -->

- **Heat, not oxidation.** Hu et al. 2014 name "high temperature" and "fermentation treatments" as two different things that happen at the panning step. See [[Fermentation]].
- **Effect on DNA.** Hu et al. 2014 give this step as a reason to use DNA markers shorter than 1 kb, which they had found are "less affected by procession treatments" [[huDevelopmentSTSCAPS2014]].
- **Fixing before drying in green tea.** In Lee et al. 2017's green-tea preparation, fixing comes first, then drying, with no fermentation [[leeDNABarcodeIdentification2017]].
