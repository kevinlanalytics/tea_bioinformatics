# Roasting

<!-- name-zh 焙火 comes from the user's template example, not from a vault source. -->

## Definition
<!-- What happens during this process? Cite the source. -->

Roasting is one of the manufacturing steps that distinguish Taiwan specialty teas. Hu et al. 2023 write that specialty teas "are crafted from whole leaves through delicate fermentation (oxidation to be exact) and roasting processes", and that Taiwan specialty teas are generally categorized into eight types depending on the manufacturing processes and cultivar flavors [[huSSRbasedMolecularDiagnosis2023]].

No source in the vault describes how roasting is done (equipment, temperature, duration).

## Distinguishing features
<!-- What separates it from similar processes? -->

- **Separate from fermentation.** Hu et al. 2023 record a roasting level and a fermentation level independently for each tea (Table 2). Oriental Beauty is heavily fermented but not roasted; Tieh-Kuan-Yin is medium-fermented but heavily roasted. See [[Fermentation]].
- **Roasting levels of nine Taiwan specialty teas** (Hu et al. 2023, Table 2):

  | Roasting level | Teas (sample ID) |
  |---|---|
  | None | Taiwan Green tea / Pi Luo Chun (ST01), Oriental Beauty (ST07), Taiwan Black tea (ST08, ST09) |
  | Light | Wenshan Paochong (ST02), High-Mountain Oolong (ST03) |
  | Medium | Tongding Oolong (ST04) |
  | Medium to heavy | Black Oolong (ST06) |
  | Heavy | Tieh-Kuan-Yin (ST05) |

- **Effect on DNA testing.** Hu et al. 2023 say an effective method to identify cultivars in processed teas was still lacking "especially in those specialty teas processed with high level of fermentation and roasting". Their teas with no or low fermentation and roasting (ST01–ST03) gave strong signals across all 12 SSR loci [[huSSRbasedMolecularDiagnosis2023]].
