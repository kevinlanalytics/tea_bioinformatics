# Lugu (鹿谷)

**County:** Nantou County
**Canonical ID:** `region_lugu`

## Geographic Notes

<!-- Elevation range, climate type, location relative to other named regions. -->

Spelling varies by source: "Lugu, Nantou" (Lee et al. 2017; Hu et al. 2023) and "Luku" in an older herbarium record (Su et al. 2009). The Chinese name 鹿谷 does not appear in any of the three sources. It comes from the entity request, as does the Luku = Lugu equivalence (see Open Questions).

- Lee et al. (2017) list Lugu, Nantou County, with Jhushan and Mingjian (also Nantou), Pinglin, Lishan, Alishan, Taitung, Yilan, and Hualien as domestic made-tea production sites. They say these sites "were selected due to their significance in the market as major tea production sites, both domestically and internationally."
- Hu et al. (2023) treat Lugu as the source of their Tongding (Tung-ting / Dongding) Oolong sample. The sample was supplied by the Lugu Farmers' Association (Table 2; Acknowledgments). The paper does not say whether "Tongding" is a place within Lugu or only a product name.
- None of the three sources gives an elevation, climate, or position relative to other named regions.

## Wild C. formosensis Populations

<!-- Is there documented presence of wild tea populations here? -->
<!-- Reference: Su et al. 2009 distribution map, NTU Experimental Forest reports. -->

Su et al. (2009) list one *Camellia formosensis* herbarium specimen from this locality under "Additional specimens examined": **NANTOU CO.: Luku, 7 Oct 1992, C. C. Liao 719 (HAST)**. No elevation is recorded for it. The paper gives an overall elevation range for the species of 800–1,800 m and places it in Nantou and five other counties.

`wild-population-present: true` rests only on this single 1992 specimen. The paper says nothing about the current status or size of a population at Luku. The Luku specimen also does not appear among the DNA samples in Su et al.'s RPB2 analysis (Table 1 / Figure 1 sampling sites). Only the "Additional specimens examined" passage names it. This should be confirmed against the PDF, because the extracted text of Table 1 is garbled (see Open Questions).

## Cultivars Grown Here

<!-- Which TTES cultivars are primarily associated with this region? Link to Cultivar notes. -->

- **Chin-Shin-Oolong (青心烏龍; landrace).** Lee et al. (2017) list made-tea samples PJK190–191 as "Lugu, Nantou — ChinShin Oolong" (Table 2). They were collected November 2011 through Dr Chia-Chang Wu at the TRES Tungding Substation. Hu et al. (2023) assigned some single-granular samples of the Lugu Tongding Oolong (ST04) to accession L001, Chin-Shin-Oolong.
- **TTES No. 12 (Chinhsuan / Jin Xuan).** Hu et al. (2023) assigned other ST04 single-granular samples to accession V012, TTES No. 12. Of the 10 single-granular samples from ST04, the authors detected "at least four unique genotype combinations," and "8 samples [were] classified into either L001 or V012 accession." The other two had genotypes that were not in the 144-cultivar database. The multi-granular data matched, except at loci C03 and D03. ST04 was one of three specialty teas (with ST05 and ST06) that showed signs of a cultivar mixture.

No cultivar notes exist yet in `Entities/Cultivars/`, so these entries are plain names, not wikilinks.

## Terroir Notes

<!-- Climate, soil, rainfall — relevant to the web app's map page context. -->

None of the sources gives climate, soil, or rainfall data for Lugu.

Product and processing data that the sources do give:

- **Hu et al. (2023), Table 2, ST04 Tongding Oolong tea.** Supplied by the Lugu Farmers' Association, harvested Spring 2020. Shape: rounded. Fermentation: medium. Roasting: medium.
- **Hu et al. (2023), DNA from ST04.** Purity ratio 260/280 of 1.80 ± 0.01 (monogranular) and 1.84 ± 0.01 (multigranular). Yield ratio 38.9 ± 6.8 (monogranular) and 72.0 ± 0.7 (multigranular).
- **Lee et al. (2017), Table 4.** The Lugu Chin Shin Oolong made tea (107_LG_PJK190–191) had an ITS2 SNP frequency of 6.9% (11/160). For Chin Shin Oolong from other Taiwanese sites the values were: Alishan 4.4%, Taitung 14.4%, Pinglin 10.6%, Hualien 8.1%, Lishan 7.8%; the domestic average was 8.8% (14.1/160). Lee et al. use these production-area SNPs as a possible provenance marker. They also note that most samples lost their characteristic SNPs when all varieties were aligned together. Lugu is not one of the samples listed in Table 5 as keeping provenance-identifying SNPs.

## Open Questions

- **Luku = Lugu?** Su et al. (2009) write "Luku" without Chinese characters. Treating it as the same place as Lugu (鹿谷) is an assumption from the entity request, not from any cited source.
- **Was the Luku specimen a DNA sample?** The existing note `Entities/Species/Camellia-formosensis.md` lists Luku among the Nantou population's molecular sampling sites. In the extracted text of Su et al. (2009), Luku appears only in "Additional specimens examined." Table 1 should be checked in the PDF.
- **Current wild population.** No source says whether wild *C. formosensis* still occurs at Lugu. The only evidence is one 1992 herbarium specimen, with no elevation recorded.
- **Tungding Substation location.** Lee et al. (2017) name a "Tungding Substation of Taiwan TRES" as the source of their 65 made-tea samples (domestic and imported) but do not give its location. Whether it is in Lugu is unconfirmed, so it is not linked to this region here.
- **"Tongding" as a place.** Hu et al. (2023) use "Tongding Oolong" as a product name. None of the three sources defines Tung-ting/Dongding (凍頂) as a place or relates it to Lugu.
- **Cultivar mix.** Is the mixed Chin-Shin-Oolong + TTES No. 12 composition in Hu et al.'s ST04 typical of Lugu Tung-ting oolong, or specific to that one Farmers' Association sample? Two ST04 granules matched no database cultivar. Their identity is unknown. Lee et al. (2017) labeled their Lugu made tea as a single variety (Chin Shin Oolong) but did not test it for mixture. The two are different samples from different years (2011 vs. Spring 2020), so this is not recorded as a source conflict.
- **Missing data.** No source gives coordinates, an elevation range, climate, soil, or rainfall.
