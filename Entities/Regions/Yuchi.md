## Geographic Notes

**Chinese name and county.** `name-zh` is 魚池鄉 (Yuchih Township), and the current administrative area is 南投縣 Nantou County, per [[taiwan-gazetteer]]. Liang-Jong Wang et al. 2024 print the same form, 南投縣魚池鄉, in an author address. The sources that name the county (Su et al. 2009, "Nantou Co."; the TBRS TTES No. 18 page, 南投) all use Nantou, so no older county name needs recording.

**Names and spellings in the sources (`aliases`):**
- **Yuchi**: the main name (`name-en`). Used by Hu et al. 2023, Jhou et al. 2024, and the TBRS TTES No. 18 page (南投魚池, "Nantou Yuchi").
- **Yuchih**: confirmed by source. Lai et al. 2001 and Su et al. 2009 use it for this place; [[taiwan-gazetteer]] gives the English label "Yuchih Township".
- **Gyoti**: confirmed by source. Su et al. 2009 write the 1935 neotype locality of *Thea formosensis* as "Taityu (Nantou Co.), Gyoti (Yuchih)", which equates the Japanese-era name with Yuchih.
- **魚池**: confirmed by source. The short form printed on the TBRS TTES No. 18 page (南投魚池), per [[TTESNo18]].

**Township rank.** The address of the Taiwan Forestry Research Institute's Lienhuachih Research Center (林業試驗所蓮華池研究中心, 南投縣魚池鄉五城村) in the author affiliations of Liang-Jong Wang et al. 2024 shows that 魚池 is a township-level unit of Nantou County. Su et al. 2009 collected a *C. furfuracea* outgroup sample at "Lienhuachi", but no source here says whether that site is inside Yuchi.

**The tea station's branch at Yuchi.** The sources name it in several ways:
- "Yuchih Substation of the Taiwan Tea Experiment Station" (Lai et al. 2001)
- "Yuchih Branch, Taiwan Tea Experiment Station" (Su et al. 2009, Table 1 footnote)
- "Yuchih Branch, Tea Research and Extension Station" (Su et al. 2009, specimen list and acknowledgments)
- "experimental tea plantation of TRES in … Yuchi" (Hu et al. 2023)

These sources all treat it as one of the station's sites outside headquarters: the older papers (Lai et al. 2001, Su et al. 2009) call it a substation or branch. Hu et al. 2023 give the Tea Research and Extension Station's address as Yangmei, Taoyuan City, and list Yuchi as one of four TRES experimental plantations (Wenshan, Yangmei, Yuchi, Taitung). The naming differences are recorded under Source Conflicts.

The caller described Yuchi as the area around Sun Moon Lake. None of the sources in the vault mention Sun Moon Lake (日月潭), so the note doesn't state that location.

No source in the vault gives Yuchi's elevation range or climate type.

## Wild C. formosensis Populations

<!-- Is there documented presence of wild tea populations here? -->
<!-- Reference: Su et al. 2009 distribution map, NTU Experimental Forest reports. -->
<!-- wild-population-present: present | absent | unknown. absent needs a source saying so; no mention = unknown. -->
<!-- wild-population-evidence: dna-sample | herbarium-only | literature-mention -->

**Herbarium records from Yuchi** (per Su et al. 2009). Su et al. list these under Nantou County in their formal treatment of *C. formosensis*:
- **Neotype of *Thea formosensis*** (the basionym of *C. formosensis*): Gyoti (Yuchih), 8 Nov 1935, flowering, S. Suzuki s.n. (TAI no. 218078). Yuchi is therefore the type locality used for the species name today.
- Yuchih, 12 Jul 1931, S. Taniguchi s.n. (TAI).
- "Shuili to Yuchih", 25 Dec 1988, B. J. Wang 15069 (TAIF).
- Yuchih Branch, Tea Research and Extension Station: 13 Jan 1966, M. Hasimoto s.n. (TAI), and 15 Nov 2005, M. H. Su 683, 687 (TAI). In Table 1, Su 683 and Su 687 are habitat code "G", meaning the germplasm garden of the Yuchih Branch. Their original localities are Derhuasia and Fenhuanshan, so these plants are held in a collection, not wild at Yuchi.

**Basis for `wild-population-present: present` / `wild-population-evidence: herbarium-only`.** The value rests only on the 1931, 1935 and 1988 herbarium records above. Su et al. 2009 Table 1 has no DNA sample from a Yuchih locality (checked against the PDF on 2026-11-10), so the evidence is herbarium-only, not dna-sample. Su et al. 2009 don't give a habitat code (wild, naturalized or planted) for those herbarium specimens, so whether they came from wild or planted trees is not stated. Jhou et al. 2024 say *C. formosensis* was grown for black tea under Japanese rule, so the early Yuchi specimens may have come from planted trees. No source in the vault reports a present-day wild stand at Yuchi, and none says wild plants are absent.

**Wild-tea germplasm held at the Yuchi station:**
- Lai et al. 2001 collected all six native wild-tea samples at the Yuchih Substation: "Shueijing", "Laitou", "Fenghuang", "Senshan", "Leye" and "Minghai". The paper classifies them as *C. sinensis* var. *assamica* f. *formosensis*.
- Hu et al. 2023, Table 1, sampled five native *C. formosensis* ("Formosana") accessions at the TRES Yuchi plantation: De-Hua-She wild tea (W001), Fong-Huang wild tea (W002), Mei-Yuan wild tea (W003), Le-Ye wild tea (W004) and Nan-Fong wild tea (W006).
- Su et al. 2009 took two germplasm-garden samples from the Yuchih Branch: F-NT-3 (Su683, Derhuasia) and F-NT-4 (Su687, Fenhuanshan). The acknowledgments thank the Yuchih Branch of TRES "for providing valuable materials".

## Cultivars Grown Here

<!-- Which TTES cultivars are primarily associated with this region? Link to Cultivar notes. -->
<!-- cultivars-grown: only cultivars a source says are grown/produced here, as [[TTES-No-N]] links or plain names. Cultivars only sampled here go in the body, not the field. -->

**Grown commercially in the area (`cultivars-grown`):**
- **[[TTES-No-18]] (臺茶18號, 紅玉 / Red Jade)**: the TBRS cultivar page calls it "one of the main cultivated varieties for black tea in the Nantou Yuchi area" (為南投魚池地區紅茶主要栽培品種之一; per [[TTESNo18]]). This is the only source in the vault that ties a named cultivar to production in Yuchi.

**Black-tea products linked to Yuchi** (no cultivar named, so not in `cultivars-grown`):
- Jhou et al. 2024 list "the Yuchi Farmers Association's black tea" among the *C. formosensis* products on the market, alongside Liouguei tea.
- Hu et al. 2023, Table 2, tested sample ST09, "Taiwan Black tea var. *assamica*", supplied by TRES-Yuchi (Summer 2018–2019). The paper doesn't say in its text which cultivar ST09 was identified as. That result appears only in Fig. 4, which the text extraction did not capture.

**Accessions sampled at the TRES Yuchi plantation** (Hu et al. 2023, Table 1). These show what is held in the germplasm plantation, not what farmers in the area grow, so they are not in `cultivars-grown`:
- **TTES-bred cultivars:** TTES No. 7 (V007, Assamica), TTES No. 18 (V018, Assamica), TTES No. 23 (V023, Sinensis), TTES No. 25 (V025, Assamica)
- **Selected line:** 55-23-6 (L057, Assamica)
- **Imported accessions:** Kimen, Kimen-2, Kimen-3, Kimen-4 (I001–I004, Sinensis); Burma No. 761 (I006F, Assamica)
- **Native *C. formosensis* accessions:** see the wild-tea germplasm list above

**Germplasm sampled at the Yuchih Substation** (Lai et al. 2001). All 37 samples in that study came from there; like the Hu et al. 2023 accessions, these are not in `cultivars-grown`:
- 21 China-tea clones, including "Kimen 1", "Kimen 2", "Shiangyuan", "Gaolu", "Chingshinoolong" and "Peimaohu"
- 3 Assam clones: "Assam Indigenous", "Manipuri", "Jaripuri"
- 7 Taiwan-bred hybrid clones: "TTE No. 11", "TTES No. 12", "TTES No. 13", "Taicha 14" to "Taicha 17"
- the six wild-tea accessions listed above

## Source Conflicts

<!-- Explicitly document any disagreement between sources on any field above. -->
<!-- Format: [Source A] says X. [Source B] says Y. Decision: pending/use A/use B. Reason: -->

- **Rank of the Yuchi unit.** [[laiAssessmentGeneticRelationships]] (2001) says "Yuchih Substation of the Taiwan Tea Experiment Station". [[suConfirmationCamelliaFormosensis]] (2009) says "Yuchih Branch". Decision: use [[suConfirmationCamelliaFormosensis]] (2009). Reason: Newest-source rule. Caveat: the difference may reflect renaming over time rather than a factual disagreement; the sources don't explain it. Hu et al. 2023, the newest, calls it only the "experimental tea plantation of TRES" and gives no rank. Superseded: "Substation" (Lai et al. 2001).
- **Parent body of the Yuchih Branch.** [[suConfirmationCamelliaFormosensis]] (2009) Table 1 footnote says "Taiwan Tea Experiment Station". The same paper's specimen list and acknowledgments say "Tea Research and Extension Station". Decision: pending. Reason: the disagreement is inside one source. (Hu et al. 2023 use "TRES", but that doesn't settle which name Su et al. meant for 2009.)
- **Where the "Minghai" wild-tea accession is held.** [[laiAssessmentGeneticRelationships]] (2001) sampled "Minghai" wild tea at the Yuchih Substation. [[huSSRbasedMolecularDiagnosis2023]] (2023) sampled "Ming-Hai wild tea" (W012) at the TRES Taitung plantation. Decision: use [[huSSRbasedMolecularDiagnosis2023]] (2023). Reason: Newest-source rule. Caveat: both can be true (duplicate plantings, or a move after 2001), and no source says the accessions are the same. Superseded: held at Yuchi (Lai et al. 2001).

## Open Questions

- **Elevation and tea-area coordinates.** No source in the vault gives an elevation range for Yuchi or the TRES Yuchi plantation. The current lat/lon are a gazetteer township point, not the centre of the tea-growing area; a source giving the plantation or tea-area location (e.g. a TRES/TBRS station page or Taiwan government GIS data) would replace it.
- **Gazetteer is not official.** [[taiwan-gazetteer]] values come from Wikidata; they should be checked against the Ministry of the Interior place-name service when that is captured.
- **Sun Moon Lake.** No source in the vault mentions Sun Moon Lake (日月潭). The caller's description of Yuchi as the area around the lake needs a citable source before it goes in the note.
- **Wild tea today.** Is there a present-day wild *C. formosensis* stand in Yuchi? Or were the 1931, 1935 and 1988 specimens (Su et al. 2009) taken from planted trees? Su et al. give no habitat code for them, so `wild-population-present: present` rests on herbarium records only.
- **Overlapping accession names.** Do these names refer to the same accessions?
  - Fenhuanshan (Su 2009), "Fenghuang" (Lai 2001) and "Fong-Huang wild tea" (Hu 2023)
  - Derhuasia (Su 2009) and "De-Hua-She wild tea" (Hu 2023)
  - "Leye" (Lai 2001) and "Le-Ye wild tea" (Hu 2023)

  All of them are recorded as held at Yuchi, but no source links them.
- **ST09 result.** Which cultivar did Hu et al. 2023 identify in the TRES-Yuchi black-tea sample ST09? The answer is only in Fig. 4, which needs to be read from the PDF. If it names a cultivar, that cultivar would qualify for `cultivars-grown` as a made-tea sample from this area.
- **Yuchi Farmers Association black tea.** Jhou et al. 2024 call it a *C. formosensis* product but don't say whether it is made from wild-type *C. formosensis* or from TTES No. 18, which is a *C. formosensis* hybrid.
- **Other cultivars.** No source names any Yuchi production cultivar besides TTES No. 18. Hu et al. 2023 sampled TTES No. 7, 23 and 25 at the Yuchi plantation, but that shows where they were sampled, not that they are grown commercially in the area.
- **Founding date.** None of the sources say when the Yuchi branch or substation was founded.
