# Liouguei (六龜)

**County:** Kaohsiung County

## Geographic Notes

<!-- Elevation range, climate type, location relative to other named regions. -->

- Spelled **Liukui** in Su et al. (2009), where it is listed under **Kaohsiung Co.** among the additional herbarium specimen localities for *Camellia formosensis*, alongside Kakuhozan, Nanfong Logging Road, Nanfongshan, Shanping, and Tona.
- Spelled **Liouguei** in Jhou et al. (2024), which names it only through the commercial product "Liouguei tea" and gives no location, county, or elevation.
- Su et al. (2009) assign the Kaohsiung County localities to the **South population** of *C. formosensis*, together with the Chiayi and Pingtung localities. Liukui itself was not one of the DNA-sampled sites, though; the Kaohsiung DNA samples came from Yuyuoshan (F-KS-1) and Sasi Logging Road (F-KS-2) (Su et al. 2009, Table 1). Neither source places Liouguei relative to other named regions.
- Neither source gives an elevation for Liouguei/Liukui. The Liukui specimen record in Su et al. (2009) has no elevation. The species-wide ranges (800–1,800 m per Su et al. 2009; 700–1,650 m per Jhou et al. 2024) describe the whole species, not this region, so they are not entered here.

## Wild C. formosensis Populations

<!-- Is there documented presence of wild tea populations here? -->
<!-- Reference: Su et al. 2009 distribution map, NTU Experimental Forest reports. -->

**Documented, based on a single herbarium record.** Su et al. (2009) list this specimen among the "Additional specimens examined" in their formal taxonomic treatment of *C. formosensis*: **Liukui, 7 Dec 1996, C. W. Huang 26 (HAST)**. Their Figure 1 marks distribution sites "based on specimens of TAI, TAIF, HAST and TNM herbaria," which places Liukui within the documented range of the species.

Limits of this evidence:
- The record is a single 1996 voucher. Neither source says anything about the population's size, condition, or whether it still exists. Su et al. (2009) do state that wild *C. formosensis* populations in general "have drastically declined due to human disturbance."
- The specimen entry does not say whether the plant was wild-growing or cultivated. `wild-population-present: true` relies on it being part of the native distribution record in the species treatment.
- The Liukui plant was not sampled for the RPB2 analysis. Its placement in the South population comes from county-level grouping, not from sequence data.
- Jhou et al. (2024) do not say whether "Liouguei tea" is made from wild trees or from cultivated *C. formosensis*.

## Cultivars Grown Here

No TTES cultivar is linked to Liouguei in the sources, so `cultivars-grown` is empty.

- **Liouguei tea**: Jhou et al. (2024) list it as one of the "various *C. formosensis* products … available on the market," alongside the Yuchi Farmers Association's black tea. The paper does not say what kind of tea it is (black, green, etc.), who produces it, or what plant material it is made from. It is a commercial product, not a registered cultivar.

- **Administrative unit.** "Kaohsiung County" is the 2009 usage of Su et al. No source in the vault gives the current administrative designation or township boundaries.
