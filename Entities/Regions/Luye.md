**What the sources say about Luye itself:** very little. Among the papers in the vault, only Hu et al. (2023) name Luye. They give "Luye, Taitung" as the provider location of specialty-tea sample **ST06 (Black Oolong tea)**, which a private tea manufacturer supplied (Table 2, footnote b; per [[huSSRbasedMolecularDiagnosis2023]]). No paper gives Luye's elevation, climate, or position relative to other named tea regions.

**Place data from the gazetteer.** [[taiwan-gazetteer]] (Wikidata Q712915) gives the Chinese name 鹿野鄉, the English label "Luye", and the current administrative area 臺東縣 Taitung County. The papers write only "Taitung" for the county, so no older county name is in use. The earlier draft value 鹿野 came from the request that created this note and is now replaced by the gazetteer form.

**Aliases.** None. The only spelling found in the sources is "Luye" (Hu 2023; the gazetteer's English label matches), so `aliases:` is empty.

**Taitung County context (not Luye-specific).** These facts concern Taitung County as a whole. No source places any of them in Luye:
- Su et al. (2009) place the "East population" of *Camellia formosensis* in Taitung County, at Yungkangshan. They describe the Taitung region as receiving strong winds from the northeast winter monsoon, winds that weaken by the time they reach central-western and southern Taiwan ([[suConfirmationCamelliaFormosensis]]).
- Lee et al. (2017) list Taitung County as one of seven domestic production areas for their made-tea samples. They describe these areas as major tea production sites ([[leeDNABarcodeIdentification2017]]).

### Tea research station in Taitung (TRES Taitung / 東部分場 Eastern Branch Station)

Three sources describe a tea-research facility in Taitung under different names. **None of them says that it is in Luye.** None of them gives a street address or a township.

Jhou et al. (2024) say that the Tea and Beverage Research Station "identified *C. formosensis* from Yongkang Mountain, Taitung County" as the breeding source for TTES No. 24 (Yongkang tea), which was named in 2019 ([[jhouInfluenceNaturalLight2024]]). This ties the "永康" in the 2019 TBRS page to a Taitung County locality. Jhou et al. do not name the branch station.

## Wild C. formosensis Populations

<!-- Is there documented presence of wild tea populations here? -->
<!-- Reference: Su et al. 2009 distribution map, NTU Experimental Forest reports. -->
<!-- wild-population-present: present | absent | unknown. absent needs a source saying so; no mention = unknown. -->

No source documents wild *C. formosensis* in Luye, and no source says it is absent there, so `wild-population-present: unknown` and `wild-population-evidence` is blank.

County-level records, none of which is located in Luye:
- Su et al. (2009) record Taitung County's wild population at Yungkangshan, which they define as the "East population." In their RPB2 data it groups with the Nantou population rather than the South population ([[suConfirmationCamelliaFormosensis]]).
- Hu et al. (2014) list W8 "Yung-Kang wild tea" as "Original from Taitung, Taiwan," classified as FY = *C. formosensis* var. *yungkangensis* ([[huDevelopmentSTSCAPS2014]]).
- Hu et al. (2023) hold Yong-Kang wild tea Nos. 2, 5, 13, and 20 at the TRES Taitung plantation. Their table records collection location, not origin ([[huSSRbasedMolecularDiagnosis2023]]).

## Cultivars Grown Here

<!-- Which TTES cultivars are primarily associated with this region? Link to Cultivar notes. -->
<!-- cultivars-grown: only cultivars a source says are grown/produced here, as [[TTES-No-N]] links or plain names. Cultivars only sampled here go in the body, not the field. -->

`cultivars-grown` lists [[TTES-No-12]], on the basis of one made-tea sample labelled as from Luye (Hu et al. 2023, [[huSSRbasedMolecularDiagnosis2023]]):
- **ST06, Black Oolong tea.** Provider "Luye, Taitung", a private tea manufacturer, spring 2021. Appearance was "rounded" (ball type), fermentation heavy, and roasting medium to heavy (Table 2).
- TTVID SSR genotyping detected "signatures of cultivar mixtures" in ST04, ST05, and ST06. For ST06, eight of ten single-granule samples were "unequivocally assigned" to **V012 (TTES No. 12)** and the other two were undetermined.
- Caveat: Table 2 records the provider's location, not where the leaf was grown, so this counts as "produced in Luye" under the field's rule rather than as evidence that TTES No. 12 is cultivated in Luye.
- ST06 had the lowest genotyping quality of the specialty teas tested, because alleles at loci C01 and C02 were hardly recognizable. DNA yield was very low: 2.9 ± 1.0 µg/g from single granules and 2.5 ± 0.2 µg/g from multiple granules (Table 5). The authors therefore recommend CTAB extraction for Black Oolong and Taiwan Black tea.

Cultivars associated with the **TRES Taitung plantation / 東部分場**, not with Luye (germplasm holdings; not in `cultivars-grown`):
- TTES No. 24 (山蘊), bred by 東部分場 per [[2019TTESNo]] and held at the Taitung plantation per [[huSSRbasedMolecularDiagnosis2023]].
- The Yong-Kang, Ming-Hai, and Shui-Li wild-tea accessions held at the Taitung plantation per [[huSSRbasedMolecularDiagnosis2023]].

Made-tea samples from **Taitung County**, location within the county unspecified (Lee et al. 2017, [[leeDNABarcodeIdentification2017]]); not in `cultivars-grown` because no source places them in Luye:
- PJK448, 450–451 (TTES No. 12, Chinhsuan) had an ITS2 SNP frequency of 4.4% (7/160). PJK450 carried a production-area-characteristic SNP, c.145A>C.
- PJK452–454 (Chin Shin Oolong) had 23 SNPs, 14.4% of the 160 sites. This was the highest frequency among both varieties' made teas across all production areas. PJK453 carried c.120G>A, c.135C>T, and c.160C>T.
- These 35 domestic made-tea samples were provided via the Tungding Substation of TRES in November 2011. They did not come from the Taitung Substation.

## Open Questions

- **Is the TRES Taitung Substation / 東部分場 in Luye?** No source says so. Lee 2017, Hu 2023, and the 2019 TBRS page give no address or township. The 089 phone code and the `taitung027` email prefix point only to Taitung in general.
- **Are "Taitung Substation of TRES" (Lee 2017), the "TRES experimental tea plantation in Taitung" (Hu 2023), and "東部分場" of TBRS (2019TTESNo) the same institution?** The sources never connect them. They also use different parent-organization names: TRES (Tea Research and Extension Station) versus 茶及飲料作物改良場 / TBRS. No source in the vault documents a rename.
- **Where was the ST06 Black Oolong leaf grown?** Hu 2023 identifies only the manufacturer's location. It does not say whether the leaf came from Luye, elsewhere in Taitung, or elsewhere. The two undetermined ST06 granules are also unidentified.
- **Wild tea in Luye.** No source records wild *C. formosensis* in Luye. The Taitung wild records are at Yungkangshan (Su 2009) and "Yung-Kang" (Hu 2014), and no source relates either one to Luye.
- **Origin of Shui-Li wild tea.** Hu 2023 gives no origin for W013 Shui-Li wild tea.
- Elevation range of the tea-growing area, a tea-area (rather than township-point) coordinate, climate, soil, and direct evidence of which cultivars are cultivated in Luye are all missing.
