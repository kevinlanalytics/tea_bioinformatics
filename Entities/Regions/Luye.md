# Luye (鹿野)

## Geographic Notes

<!-- Elevation range, climate type, location relative to other named regions. -->

**What the sources say about Luye itself:** very little. The only source in the vault that names Luye is Hu et al. (2023). It gives "Luye, Taitung" as the provider location of specialty-tea sample **ST06 (Black Oolong tea)**, which a private tea manufacturer supplied (Table 2, footnote b; per [[huSSRbasedMolecularDiagnosis2023]]). No source gives Luye's elevation, climate, or position relative to other named tea regions. The Chinese name 鹿野 came from the request that created this note and does not appear in any source (see Open Questions).

**Taitung County context (not Luye-specific).** These facts concern Taitung County as a whole. No source places any of them in Luye:
- Su et al. (2009) place the "East population" of *Camellia formosensis* in Taitung County, at Yungkangshan. They describe the Taitung region as receiving strong winds from the northeast winter monsoon, winds that weaken by the time they reach central-western and southern Taiwan ([[suConfirmationCamelliaFormosensis]]).
- Lee et al. (2017) list Taitung County as one of seven domestic production areas for their made-tea samples. They describe these areas as major tea production sites ([[leeDNABarcodeIdentification2017]]).

### Tea research station in Taitung (TRES Taitung / 東部分場 Eastern Branch Station)

Three sources describe a tea-research facility in Taitung under different names. **None of them says that it is in Luye.** None of them gives a street address or a township.

| Name as given | Source | What the source says |
|---|---|---|
| "Taitung Substation of Taiwan TRES" / "Taitung Substation of TRES" | Lee et al. 2017 ([[leeDNABarcodeIdentification2017]]) | Housed the 113 reference tea varieties sampled for ITS2 barcoding: 20 TRES breeding varieties, 72 small-leaf (var. *sinensis*), 9 large-leaf (var. *assamica*), and 12 Taiwanese native wild tea plants. Samples were provided by Mr Sang-Shun Wu, who also helped identify the plants. The apical bud plus the two youngest leaves came from a single strain of each variety. Samples were prepared in March 2008. The paper also says its "cultured tea library was established using tea plants supported by the Taitung Substation of TRES." |
| "experimental tea plantation of TRES in … Taitung" | Hu et al. 2023 ([[huSSRbasedMolecularDiagnosis2023]]) | One of four TRES experimental plantations (Wenshan, Yangmei, Yuchi, Taitung) that supplied fresh leaves for the 144-cultivar SSR database. Table 1 lists these accessions as collected at Taitung: **V024 (TTES No. 24)** and the Formosana-group native accessions **W008–W011 (Yong-Kang wild tea Nos. 2, 5, 13, 20), W012 (Ming-Hai wild tea), and W013 (Shui-Li wild tea)**. |
| 東部分場 (Eastern Branch Station) of 農業部茶及飲料作物改良場 (TBRS) | TBRS web page, 2019 ([[2019TTESNo]]) | Credited with breeding TTES No. 24 (臺茶24號, 山蘊) from an introduction of a Taiwan native wild tea, the "永康變種" (Yongkang variant). The page was titled "東部分場育成臺灣原生山茶品種-臺茶24號(山蘊)" and the cultivar was named in ROC year 108 (2019). Two of the three listed consultation experts are at 東部分場: 蕭建興 (ext. 210) and 余錦安 (ext. 220). Jhou et al. (2024) say that the Tea and Beverage Research Station "identified *C. formosensis* from Yongkang Mountain, Taitung County" as the breeding source for TTES No. 24 (Yongkang tea), which was named in 2019 ([[jhouInfluenceNaturalLight2024]]). This ties the "永康" in the 2019 TBRS page to a Taitung County locality. Jhou et al. do not name the branch station.

## Wild C. formosensis Populations

<!-- Is there documented presence of wild tea populations here? -->
<!-- Reference: Su et al. 2009 distribution map, NTU Experimental Forest reports. -->

No source documents wild *C. formosensis* in Luye. `wild-population-present: false` records that no source documents a population there. It does not mean a source confirmed that none exists.

County-level records, none of which is located in Luye:
- Su et al. (2009) record Taitung County's wild population at Yungkangshan, which they define as the "East population." In their RPB2 data it groups with the Nantou population rather than the South population ([[suConfirmationCamelliaFormosensis]]).
- Hu et al. (2014) list W8 "Yung-Kang wild tea" as "Original from Taitung, Taiwan," classified as FY = *C. formosensis* var. *yungkangensis* ([[huDevelopmentSTSCAPS2014]]).
- Hu et al. (2023) hold Yong-Kang wild tea Nos. 2, 5, 13, and 20 at the TRES Taitung plantation. Their table records collection location, not origin ([[huSSRbasedMolecularDiagnosis2023]]).

## Cultivars Grown Here

<!-- Which TTES cultivars are primarily associated with this region? Link to Cultivar notes. -->

`cultivars-grown` is left empty on purpose. No source says which cultivars are grown in Luye.

The one cultivar-level data point tied to Luye comes from Hu et al. (2023). It concerns a processed product and does not show where the leaf was grown:
- **ST06, Black Oolong tea.** Supplied by a private manufacturer in Luye, Taitung, in spring 2021. Appearance was "rounded" (ball type), fermentation heavy, and roasting medium to heavy (Table 2).
- TTVID SSR genotyping detected "signatures of cultivar mixtures" in ST04, ST05, and ST06. For ST06, eight of ten single-granule samples were "unequivocally assigned" to **V012 (TTES No. 12)** and the other two were undetermined.
- ST06 had the lowest genotyping quality of the specialty teas tested, because alleles at loci C01 and C02 were hardly recognizable. DNA yield was very low: 2.9 ± 1.0 µg/g from single granules and 2.5 ± 0.2 µg/g from multiple granules (Table 5). The authors therefore recommend CTAB extraction for Black Oolong and Taiwan Black tea.

Cultivars associated with the **TRES Taitung plantation / 東部分場**, not with Luye:
- TTES No. 24 (山蘊), bred by 東部分場 per [[2019TTESNo]] and held at the Taitung plantation per [[huSSRbasedMolecularDiagnosis2023]].
- The Yong-Kang, Ming-Hai, and Shui-Li wild-tea accessions held at the Taitung plantation per [[huSSRbasedMolecularDiagnosis2023]].

Made-tea samples from **Taitung County**, location within the county unspecified (Lee et al. 2017, [[leeDNABarcodeIdentification2017]]):
- PJK448, 450–451 (TTES No. 12, Chinhsuan) had an ITS2 SNP frequency of 4.4% (7/160). PJK450 carried a production-area-characteristic SNP, c.145A>C.
- PJK452–454 (Chin Shin Oolong) had 23 SNPs, 14.4% of the 160 sites. This was the highest frequency among both varieties' made teas across all production areas. PJK453 carried c.120G>A, c.135C>T, and c.160C>T.
- These 35 domestic made-tea samples were provided via the Tungding Substation of TRES in November 2011. They did not come from the Taitung Substation.

## Open Questions

- **Is the TRES Taitung Substation / 東部分場 in Luye?** No source says so. Lee 2017, Hu 2023, and the 2019 TBRS page give no address or township. The 089 phone code and the `taitung027` email prefix point only to Taitung in general.
- **Are "Taitung Substation of TRES" (Lee 2017), the "TRES experimental tea plantation in Taitung" (Hu 2023), and "東部分場" of TBRS (2019TTESNo) the same institution?** The sources never connect them. They also use different parent-organization names: TRES (Tea Research and Extension Station) versus 茶及飲料作物改良場 / TBRS. No source in the vault documents a rename.
- **Chinese name.** 鹿野 is not attested in any source. Hu 2023 gives only the romanization "Luye."
- **Where was the ST06 Black Oolong leaf grown?** Hu 2023 identifies only the manufacturer's location. It does not say whether the leaf came from Luye, elsewhere in Taitung, or elsewhere.
- **Wild tea in Luye.** No source records wild *C. formosensis* in Luye. The Taitung wild records are at Yungkangshan (Su 2009) and "Yung-Kang" (Hu 2014), and no source relates either one to Luye.
- **Origin of Ming-Hai and Shui-Li wild tea.** Hu 2014 lists W5 Ming-Hai wild tea as "Original from Kaohsiung." Hu 2023 lists W012 Ming-Hai wild tea at "Taitung," but that column records the plantation where leaves were collected, not origin, so this is probably not a real conflict. Hu 2023 gives no origin for W013 Shui-Li wild tea.
- **Small internal inconsistency in Lee 2017.** The text gives the Taitung Chin Shin Oolong figure as "14.4% (23/156) of the total 160 sites." The SNP-frequency table gives 14.4 (23/160).
- Coordinates, elevation range, climate, soil, and the cultivars actually grown in Luye are all missing.
