- **Chinese name (inferred).** `name-zh: 眉原山` comes from [[taiwan-gazetteer]]: Wikidata's summit "Meiyuan Shan" (Q31600306, 眉原山, 24.1138, 120.9869) lies about 0.7 km from the tree that Wang et al. (2024) locate at Meiyuanshan. The identification rests only on that coordinate match and the shared romanisation. No literature source prints Chinese characters for the site.
- **Spellings and aliases.**
  - "Meiyuanshan" (the note name) is used in Table 1 of Su et al. (2009) and throughout Wang et al. (2024) ([[liang-jongwangPlastomeCamelliaFormosensis2024]]).
  - "Meiyuenshan": **confirmed by a source.** Su et al. (2009) ([[suConfirmationCamelliaFormosensis]]) use this spelling in their list of additional specimens examined (checked against the PDF, p. 482). Both spellings are filed under NANTOU CO., so this note treats them as one locality. That they are the same place is assumed from the shared county and near-identical spelling; the paper does not say so.
  - "Meiyuan Shan": the English label of the Wikidata summit in [[taiwan-gazetteer]]. Its link to this locality is the same coordinate-match **inference** as the Chinese name.
- **County.** Both papers place it in Nantou County (Su et al. 2009, specimen list; Wang et al. 2024, methods). [[taiwan-gazetteer]] records no administrative area for the summit. No source uses an older county name. No source names the township.
- **Population grouping.** Su et al. (2009) place the Meiyuanshan samples (F-NT-1, F-NT-2) in their **Nantou population** of *C. formosensis*, together with Derhuasia, Fenhuanshan, Yuchih, the Yuchih Branch germplasm garden, Luku and Shuili-to-Yuchih. In the RPB2 tree, the Nantou population falls in one subclade with the East (Taitung) population, separate from the South population.
- **Position on the Su et al. (2009) map.** Figure 1 labels DNA sampling sites by running number. The point labelled "1,2" under NT (F-NT-1/F-NT-2, the Meiyuanshan samples) is the northernmost Nantou sampling point on that map (read from the PDF figure). The paper gives no coordinates for it.
- **Elevation.** The only elevations come from specimen labels in Su et al. (2009): **1,500 m** (A. Tanimura s.n., 12 Jan 1935, TAI) and **1,250 m** (C. H. Tsou 2132, 2134, 2137, 2139, 30 Mar 2005). The frontmatter `elevation-min-m`/`elevation-max-m` (1250/1500, `elevation-basis: specimen-label`) show the range of these collection elevations, not a surveyed elevation range for the locality. Wang et al. (2024) give no elevation for their 2021 collection point. Gazetteer elevations are not used.

## Wild C. formosensis Populations

<!-- Is there documented presence of wild tea populations here? -->
<!-- Reference: Su et al. 2009 distribution map, NTU Experimental Forest reports. -->
<!-- wild-population-present: present | absent | unknown. absent needs a source saying so; no mention = unknown. -->
<!-- wild-population-evidence: dna-sample | herbarium-only | literature-mention -->

**Documented (`present`; evidence `dna-sample`).** Both sources record wild *C. formosensis* here, and the records run from 1935 to 2021. Su et al. (2009) DNA-sampled plants coded wild, and Wang et al. (2024) sequenced a plastome from a tree here.

- **Su et al. (2009), Table 1:** two of the 13 *C. formosensis* samples in the RPB2 study came from Meiyuanshan, and both are coded habitat **"W" (wild)**:
  - F-NT-1: voucher Tsou 2133; GenBank EU849031 (introns 12–16), EU849066 (intron 23)
  - F-NT-2: voucher Tsou 2135; GenBank EU849032 (introns 12–16), EU849067 (intron 23)
  - The other two Nantou samples (Derhuasia, Fenhuanshan) are coded "G" (Yuchih Branch germplasm garden). That makes Meiyuanshan the only wild-collected Nantou site in the molecular sampling.
- **Su et al. (2009), additional specimens examined (Nantou Co.):** "Meiyuenshan, elev. 1,500 m, 12 Jan 1935, *A. Tanimura s.n.* (TAI); same loc., 8 Oct 1935, *S. Sasaki s.n.* (TAI); same loc., 8 Nov 1935, *S. Sasaki s.n.* (TAI); same loc., 9 Nov 1935, *S. Suzuki s.n.* (TAI); same loc., 16 Jan 1966, *M. Hasimoto s.n.* (TAI); same loc., 23 Apr 1966, *M. T. Kao 6668, 6676* (TAI); same loc., elev. 1,250 m, 30 Mar 2005, *C. H. Tsou 2132, 2134, 2137, 2139*." No herbarium is given for the 2005 Tsou collections.
- **Wang et al. (2024):** fresh leaves of *C. formosensis* were collected at Meiyuanshan in **March 2021** by C. K. Hsu and F. H. Chen. Voucher **CamW003** is at TAIF, and DNA voucher DNACamW003 is at the Laboratory of Forest Biodiversity, Taiwan Forestry Research Institute. This sample produced the first complete *C. formosensis* plastome (156,986 bp; GenBank ON422328). The Fig. 1A caption reads: "A 10 m high old tree was discovered in Meiyuanshan, Nantou County, Taiwan." The paper does not explicitly call this tree wild-growing, though its title and framing are about "Taiwanese wild tea."
- **Population status:** neither source gives a size or condition for the Meiyuanshan population. Su et al. (2009) say only that wild Taiwanese wild tea populations in general "have drastically declined due to human disturbance."

## Source Conflicts

<!-- Explicitly document any disagreement between sources on any field above. -->
<!-- Format: [Source A] says X. [Source B] says Y. Decision: pending/use A/use B. Reason: -->

No conflicts between sources on this note's fields, so `conflicts:` is empty. The items below were listed as conflicts before. They are kept here as notes because they are species-level points (see `issues.md` ISS-019) that belong in [[Camellia-formosensis]]:

- **Tree height: not a conflict for this note.** Wang et al. (2024) ([[liang-jongwangPlastomeCamelliaFormosensis2024]], Fig. 1A) report "a 10 m high old tree" of *C. formosensis* at Meiyuanshan. Su et al. (2009) ([[suConfirmationCamelliaFormosensis]], formal description) describe the species as "a small, evergreen tree, up to 8 m high." One measures a single tree and the other describes the species, so they do not give competing values for a field of this region, and the newest-source rule is not applied here. The 10 m tree exceeds the 8 m species maximum. Whether that should change the species description is for the species note to decide.
- **Flowering period (species-level).** Wang et al. (2024, Fig. 1B caption) give flowering "from September to February." Su et al. (2009) give "Flowering from September to January." Wang et al.'s caption does not say whether this was observed at Meiyuanshan specifically. It is recorded here only because the figure belongs to the Meiyuanshan specimen work.
- **Spelling only, not a conflict:** "Meiyuanshan" (Su et al. 2009 Table 1; Wang et al. 2024) vs. "Meiyuenshan" (Su et al. 2009 specimen list). Both are kept in `aliases:` or as the note name.

## Open Questions

- **Chinese name confirmation.** `name-zh: 眉原山` is inferred from a gazetteer coordinate match ([[taiwan-gazetteer]]). No literature source names it. A source that prints the Chinese name for the collection site (or an official MOI place-name record) would confirm it.
- **Township.** No source names the township, and the gazetteer records no administrative area for the summit.
- **Link to Lee et al. 2017 "Mei Yuan".** [[leeDNABarcodeIdentification2017]] lists a native wild tea accession **No. 98 "Mei Yuan"** (Table 1), which falls in a Taiwanese wild *Camellia* cluster. The paper does not say whether this accession came from Meiyuanshan, so it is not used as a source or alias here. Someone should check.
- **Tsou vouchers.** Table 1 vouchers Tsou 2133 and 2135 (Meiyuanshan, wild) are not in the specimen list. The specimen list's 2005 Meiyuenshan collection is Tsou 2132, 2134, 2137, 2139. The numbers suggest one collecting trip, but Su et al. (2009) do not say so, and they do not give a herbarium for the 2005 vouchers. Is 1,250 m the right elevation for the DNA samples?
- **One tree or several?** We don't know whether the 2021 tree sampled by Wang et al. (the 10 m "old tree") is the same plant as, or near, the plants sampled in 1935–2005. We also don't know how many individuals the population has today.
- **Locality extent.** No source defines the area or boundaries of "Meiyuanshan." The lat/lon is a single specimen point, and the elevation range covers only two labelled collections.
- **Conservation.** No source gives a protection designation or current population trend for this site.
