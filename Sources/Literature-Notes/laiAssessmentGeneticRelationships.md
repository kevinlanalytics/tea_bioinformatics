# An assessment of genetic relationships in cultivated tea clones and native wild tea in Taiwan using RAPD and ISSR markers

> **Authors:** Jou-Ann Lai, Wei-Chen Yang, Ju-Ying Hsiao
> **Year:** 2001
> **Journal:** 
> **DOI:** 
> [Open in Zotero](zotero://select/library/items/TLVM9PUN)

## Abstract

Tea (Camellia sinensis [L.] O. Kuntze) is an important beverage crop in Taiwan. Most of the cultivated clones were introduced from China and India though some native wild teas are distributed in the mountains of central and southern Taiwan. In this study, 37 tea samples were evaluated using RAPD and ISSR markers. The samples comprised 21 clones of China, 3 clones of Assam, 7 hybrid clones between China and Assam tea, and 6 individual samples of native Taiwanese wild tea. A total of 53 and 56 polymorphic RAPD and ISSR markers respectively, were scored. The results of cluster analysis based on RAPDs revealed that three major groups could be recognized, i.e., cultivars of China tea and the cultivars developed in Taiwan from hybridization and selection; Assam tea; native Taiwanese wild tea. The native Taiwanese wild teas were, however, most distant in the clustering tree. In the ISSR dendrogram, Taiwanese native wild teas clustered closely with Assam tea then with China tea and the Taiwanese hybrid cultivars. The population gene diversity of the native wild tea was found to be the highest among the three populations studied. Analysis of molecular variance (AMOVA) revealed that the variance component within groups was larger than that among groups. The correlation coefﬁcient between similarity matrices based on RAPD and ISSR was 0.811. A Mantel test revealed that the correlation was highly signiﬁcant (p<0.001), indicating good congruence between the results of these two molecular markers.

## Key Claims Relevant to Tea KG

- Sample set: 37 tea samples total — 21 China tea clones, 3 Assam tea clones, 7 China x Assam hybrid clones bred in Taiwan, and 6 individual native Taiwanese wild tea samples — all collected from the Yuchih Substation of the Taiwan Tea Experiment Station.
- RAPD analysis: 140 decanucleotide primers (Operon kits A, B, C, E, M, Q, V) were screened; 12 primers gave reproducible bands, yielding 53 polymorphic bands (average 4.4 bands/primer).
- ISSR analysis: 20 3'-anchored primers were screened; 6 primers gave reproducible bands, yielding 56 polymorphic bands (average 9.3 bands/primer).
- RAPD-based UPGMA clustering (Dice similarity) resolved three major groups at similarity index 0.5: (1) China tea cultivars + Taiwan-bred China x Assam hybrid cultivars, (2) Assam tea, (3) native Taiwanese wild tea — the wild tea group was the most distant/isolated cluster. Cophenetic correlation coefficient = 0.870.
- Within the native wild tea RAPD subcluster, two subgroups separated at similarity 0.6: red-leaf-budded "Shueijing" + "Laitou" vs. the remaining non-red-leafed wild tea accessions.
- ISSR-based UPGMA clustering instead resolved only two major groups at similarity index 0.5: (1) all China tea + hybrid cultivars, (2) native wild tea clustered together with Assam tea (with a further split at similarity 0.55 into red-leafed wild tea Shueijing/Laitou vs. Assam cultivars + non-red-leafed wild tea). Cophenetic correlation coefficient = 0.843.
- Combining RAPD + ISSR data gave a dendrogram with two major groups, similar to the ISSR-only result.
- Hybrid clone "TTE No. 11" clustered with the China tea group in both RAPD and ISSR analyses despite being morphologically more similar to Assam tea.
- RAPD: highest pairwise similarity 1.000 between "Heimaohu" and "Dananuanpeimaohu" (identical RAPD profiles, i.e., RAPD could not distinguish them); lowest similarity 0.238 between "Shiangyuan" and "Laitou". ISSR could distinguish all 37 samples, including Heimaohu vs. Dananuanpeimaohu (highest ISSR similarity 0.923); lowest ISSR similarity 0.270 between "Minghai" (native wild tea) and "Gaolu".
- Nei's gene diversity (Hs): RAPD — China tea 0.197, Assam tea 0.074, native wild tea 0.280 (highest of the three groups). ISSR — China tea 0.181, Assam tea 0.114, native wild tea 0.253 (highest).
- AMOVA (RAPD, WINAMOVA 1.55, Excoffier et al. 1992 formula, groups = China/Assam/native wild tea, hybrids excluded): among-group variance component 5.099 (46.69% of total), within-group (among individuals) 5.822 (53.31%); both P<0.001 (10,000 permutations).
- AMOVA (ISSR): among-group variance component 5.021 (44.11% of total), within-group 6.362 (55.89%); both P<0.001.
- Pearson correlation coefficient between RAPD and ISSR similarity matrices = 0.811; Mantel test confirmed this correlation was highly significant (P<0.001), indicating good congruence between the two marker systems.
- Clones "Peimaohu", "Dananuanpeimaohu", and "Tutuyykengpeimaohu" were all introduced from Fukien (Fujian), China, and had been suspected to be the same clone; RAPD and ISSR distinguished them (pairwise similarities 0.68–0.74 for RAPD, 0.58–0.76 for ISSR).
- Pedigree claim: clones "Taicha 14", "Taicha 15", "Taicha 16", and "Taicha 17" were developed (bred/selected) from "Peimaohu". "Taicha 14", "Taicha 15", and "Taicha 17" clustered with "Peimaohu" in both RAPD and ISSR dendrograms, reflecting this pedigree. "Taicha 16" did not cluster with "Peimaohu" but still showed high similarity to it (RAPD 0.80, ISSR 0.77).
- Clones "TTES No. 12" and "TTES No. 13" are morphologically indistinguishable but differ in parentage, cultivation method, and tea quality; RAPD and ISSR profiles could separate them, confirming genetic difference consistent with distinct parentage.
- "Shiangyuan" and "Gaolu" are morphologically similar triploids that rarely flower; "Gaolu" was introduced from Japan while "Shiangyuan" was introduced from Fukien, China. Their RAPD similarity (0.58) and ISSR similarity (0.60) indicate substantial genetic difference despite morphological similarity.
- "Shiangyuan" has its highest similarity with "Sheauyehtieeguanin" (RAPD 0.81, ISSR 0.86); both were introduced from Fukien, China, and share similar leaf morphology.
- Historical/introduction claims: the first tea cultivar grown in Taiwan was introduced from Fukien (China) between 1796 and 1820; more China-tea cultivars followed; from 1926 onward some new cultivars were introduced from India (Assam type).
- Taxonomic framework used: China tea = Camellia sinensis var. sinensis (L.) O. Kuntze; Assam tea = C. sinensis var. assamica (Mast.) Kitam.; native Taiwanese wild tea = C. sinensis var. assamica f. formosensis Kitam., distributed in the central and southern mountains of Taiwan.
- Results (especially ISSR) support the existing taxonomic treatment of native Taiwanese wild tea as a form of the Assam variety, given its close genetic clustering with Assam tea.
- The RAPD-based genetic distinctness of native wild tea from cultivated tea groups is interpreted as evidence that native wild tea is genuinely native to Taiwan rather than an escape from cultivation.
- Native wild tea's germplasm is highlighted as a potentially valuable genetic resource for tea breeding programs and as deserving conservation, given its comparatively high within-group gene diversity despite the small sample (n=6) relative to China tea (n=21).
- Cambod tea (C. assamica ssp. lasiocalyx Planchon ex Watt) was explicitly not included in this study, unlike Wachira et al. (1995), which analyzed China, Assam, and Cambod tea together.
- Comparison to prior work: Wachira et al. (1995) found some seemingly China-type clones clustering with Assam tea (putative China x Assam hybrids retaining China morphology), analogous to this paper's "TTE No. 11" finding; Wachira et al. (1995) reported 30% of RAPD diversity among populations vs. 70% within (Shannon's index) for China/Assam/Cambod tea; Paul et al. (1997) found 21% among-population vs. 79% within-population diversity (AFLP, Shannon's index) for Indian and Kenyan tea.

## Methods Used

- RAPD (Random Amplified Polymorphic DNA) markers: 140 decanucleotide primers from Operon Technology Inc. kits A, B, C, E, M, Q, and V screened; 12 primers used, yielding 53 scored polymorphic bands (avg. 4.4 bands/primer); thermal profile 94°C/2min, 44 cycles of 94°C/30s–36°C/30s–72°C/2min, 72°C/5min.
- ISSR (Inter-Simple Sequence Repeat) markers: 20 3'-anchored primers screened; 6 primers used (including primer "814.1" and UBC830, UBC825, UBC840, UBC842, "17901"), yielding 56 scored polymorphic bands (avg. 9.3 bands/primer); thermal profile 94°C/3min, 45 cycles of 94°C/40s–50°C/45s–72°C/100s, 72°C/5min.
- DNA extraction following Kobayashi et al. (1998) method for plant/woody tissue.
- PCR performed on a PTC-100 Thermal Cycler (MJ Research Inc.); DNA polymerase from HT Biotechnology (UK); negative (no-DNA) control included each run; amplifications repeated at least twice per sample.
- Gel electrophoresis: 1.5% agarose, 0.5X TBE buffer, 200V for 2h, ethidium bromide staining, documented on Polaroid 667 film; fragment sizing against a Bio100 DNA ladder.
- Only intensely stained, unambiguous, reproducible bands scored as binary presence/absence data.
- Dice's (1945) coefficient used to build similarity matrices from binary marker data.
- UPGMA (unweighted pair group method using arithmetic averages) cluster analysis on RAPD, ISSR, and combined RAPD+ISSR data.
- Cophenetic correlation coefficient calculated to assess dendrogram fit to the similarity matrix (RAPD: 0.870; ISSR: 0.843).
- Pearson's correlation coefficient between RAPD and ISSR similarity matrices, tested for significance with a Mantel test (per Sokal and Rohlf 1995).
- AMOVA (Analysis of Molecular Variance) via WINAMOVA 1.55, following Excoffier et al. (1992) distance formula D=N(1−(N11/N)); samples grouped into China tea, Assam tea, and native wild tea (hybrid clones excluded from this grouping); significance assessed via 10,000 random permutations.
- Nei's (1973) gene diversity (Hs) computed per group using POPGENE 3.2 software (Yeh et al. 1999), assuming Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium.
- Total sample size: 37 tea samples (21 China tea, 3 Assam tea, 7 hybrid clones, 6 native wild tea individuals).

## Conflicts With Other Sources

- **Genetic diversity ranking directly contradicts [[huDevelopmentSTSCAPS2014]] (Hu et al. 2014).** This paper (RAPD/ISSR) finds native Taiwanese wild tea has the *highest* population gene diversity of the three groups studied (Hs: RAPD 0.280, ISSR 0.253, vs. China tea 0.197/0.181 and Assam tea 0.074/0.114). Hu et al. (2014, STS/CAPS) found the opposite — cultivated *C. sinensis* (sinensis/assamica types) more diverse than wild *C. formosensis*. Hu et al. (2014) explicitly attributes the discrepancy to two of this paper's "wild tea" samples (Laitou, Shueijing) later being identified by Su (2007) as *Camellia furfuracea* rather than true *C. formosensis*, and to this paper's Assam-type samples all being India-sourced rather than Taiwan-grown assamica germplasm.
- **Taxonomic treatment of Taiwanese wild tea predates the species-level reclassification in [[suConfirmationCamelliaFormosensis]] (Su et al.).** This paper (2001) treats native Taiwanese wild tea as *Camellia sinensis var. assamica f. formosensis* Kitam. — a form nested under the assamica variety — and explicitly interprets its ISSR clustering results as supporting that treatment. Su et al. later elevated the same taxon to the independent species *Camellia formosensis*, a reclassification adopted by every other molecular paper in this vault ([[huDevelopmentSTSCAPS2014]], [[huSSRbasedMolecularDiagnosis2023]], [[jhouInfluenceNaturalLight2024]], [[liang-jongwangPlastomeCamelliaFormosensis2024]]).
