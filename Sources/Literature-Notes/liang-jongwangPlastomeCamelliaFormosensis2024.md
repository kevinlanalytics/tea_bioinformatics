# The Plastome of Camellia formosensis (Theaceae) Reconfirms the Endemism of Taiwanese Wild Tea

## Abstract

In this study, we sequenced and assembled the complete plastid genome of Camellia formosensis. The complete plastome of C. formosensis is 156,986 base pairs (bp) in length, comprising a pair of inverted repeats (IRa and IRb, 26,085 bp), one large single-copy (LSC, 86,554 bp) region, and one small single-copy (SSC, 18262 bp) region. The plastome’s nucleotide composition is as follows: A: 31.1%; T: 31.6%; C: 19.0%; and G: 18.3%. A total of 132 genes are predicted within the plastome, including 87 protein-coding genes (PCGs), 37 transfer RNAs (tRNAs) genes, and 8 ribosomal RNAs (rRNAs) genes. C. formosensis is sister to the clade containing [((C. ptilophylla + ((C. sinensis ‘Sangmok’ + (C. leptophylla + C. sinensis var. sinensis)) + ((C. sinensis ‘Longjian’ + C. sinensis ‘Dahongpao’))], suggesting that Taiwanese wild tea is phylogenetically distinct from the beverage tea C. sinensis. The phylogenetic result confirms that C. formosensis is a distinct species. The results indicate that the two subgenera of the genus Camellia are not monophyletic groups, suggesting that the current infrageneric classification of Camellia based on morphology should be reconsidered and revised using more genomic data and more taxa. The plastome data of C. formosensis from this study will provide valuable molecular markers for further research, helping to accurately identify similar Camellia species, as well as supporting future studies of population genetics, speciation, and conservation of the species in Taiwan.

## Key Claims Relevant to Tea KG

- The complete plastid genome (plastome) of *Camellia formosensis* is 156,986 bp, deposited as GenBank accession ON422328.
- The plastome has the typical quadripartite structure: a pair of inverted repeats (IRa/IRb, 26,085 bp each), one large single-copy (LSC) region of 86,554 bp, and one small single-copy (SSC) region of 18,262 bp.
- Nucleotide composition of the plastome: A 31.1%, T 31.6%, C 19.0%, G 18.3%.
- 132 genes were annotated in the plastome: 87 protein-coding genes (PCGs), 37 tRNA genes, and 8 rRNA genes.
- Average sequencing coverage depth of the assembly was >500X.
- 13 genes show cis-splicing (rps16, atpF, rpoC1, ycf3, clpP, petB, petD, rpl16, rpl2 [x2 copies], ndhA, ndhB [x2 copies]); rps12 is a trans-spliced gene with one exon in the LSC region and a duplicated exon in the inverted repeats.
- Phylogenomic analysis (24 Camellia plastomes + Polyspora speciosa outgroup) places C. formosensis as sister to the entire clade containing [((C. ptilophylla + ((C. sinensis 'Sangmok' + (C. leptophylla + C. sinensis var. sinensis)) + (C. sinensis 'Longjian' + C. sinensis 'Dahongpao')))], i.e., outside/basal to all C. sinensis cultivars and closely related species sampled.
- This topology indicates Taiwanese wild tea (C. formosensis) is phylogenetically distinct from beverage tea C. sinensis, reconfirming C. formosensis as a distinct species (supporting the hypothesis of Su et al. 2009).
- ML, BI, and MP methods all recovered the same topology (though nodal support values differed between methods); MP consensus tree length = 1883, consistency index = 0.77, retention index = 0.90.
- The genus Camellia's two traditionally recognized subgenera (Thea and Camellia) are not recovered as monophyletic groups in this analysis, consistent with prior studies (Vijayan et al. 2009; Yang et al. 2013; Huang et al. 2014).
- Section Thea (per Ming 2000's classification) is shown to be polyphyletic based on the scattered phylogenetic positions of C. taliensis, C. tachangensis, and C. danzaiensis.
- The authors argue the current infrageneric (subgenus/section) classification of Camellia, based on morphology (Ming 2000), should be reconsidered/revised using more genomic data and broader taxon sampling.
- Multiple/diverse phylogenetic origins observed among C. sinensis accessions are proposed to result from complex artificial introduction and hybridization over the long history of Chinese tea cultivation, rather than reflecting deep natural lineages.
- Plastome inheritance in C. sinensis is maternal (citing Kaundun and Matsumoto 2011), which underlies the interpretation of plastome-based phylogenetic placement as maternal-lineage evidence.
- Key diagnostic morphological character separating C. formosensis from C. sinensis: glabrous ovate-lanceolate buds in C. formosensis vs. pubescent buds in C. sinensis (illustrated with cultivar Chin-Shin-Oolong).
- Taxonomic history: originally described as Thea formosensis Masamune et Suzuki (1937) based on glabrous buds; Kitamura (1950) treated it as a variety of C. sinensis; Ming (2000) treated it as a synonym/form of C. sinensis; molecular data from nuclear RPB2 gene introns (12-16, 23) supported recognizing it as an independent species, leading to the new combination Camellia formosensis (Masamune et Suzuki) M.H.Su, C.F.Hsieh et C.H.Tsou (Su et al. 2007, 2009).
- C. formosensis is distributed in the central, southern, and eastern regions of Taiwan (Su et al. 2009), i.e., presented as endemic to Taiwan.
- The voucher specimen (accession CamW003) came from a ~10 m tall tree; flowering period observed September to February.

## Methods Used

- Field sampling: fresh leaves of C. formosensis collected at Meiyuanshan, Nantou County, Taiwan, in March 2021; voucher specimen (accession CamW003) deposited in the Herbarium of Taiwan Forestry Research Institute (TAIF).
- Total genomic DNA extraction using a GeneMark Plant Genomic DNA Purification Kit (GMBIOLAB); DNA voucher (accession DNACamW003) deposited in the Laboratory of Forest Biodiversity, Taiwan Forestry Research Institute.
- Sequencing library construction with the Celero DNA-Seq Library Preparation Kit (Tecan).
- Paired-end (2 x 150 bp) whole-genome sequencing on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000.
- Sequence quality analysis, trimming, and de novo assembly using CLC Genomics Workbench ver. 12.0.02 (QIAGEN).
- Target-contig identification via BLAST against reference plastomes of C. sinensis var. sinensis (NCBI acc. KJ806281), C. leptophylla (KJ806275), and C. ptilophylla (MG797642); three targeted contigs (e-value = 0) selected and manually aligned/adjusted/combined to assemble the complete plastome.
- Assembly accuracy verified via a coverage depth map generated by mapping clean reads back to the assembled plastome (average depth >500X).
- Gene prediction and annotation using the GeSeq Web Server, verified by alignment against the three reference Camellia plastomes above.
- Plastome map visualization with OGDRAW; cis-splicing and trans-splicing gene schematic maps produced with Chloroplast Genome Viewer (CPGView).
- Phylogenetic dataset: 24 Camellia plastomes (the newly sequenced C. formosensis plus 23 other Camellia taxa retrieved from GenBank/NCBI) plus Polyspora speciosa as outgroup, sampled from the section Thea framework following Ming (2000) infrageneric classification.
- Sequence preparation: inverted repeat regions concatenated as a single copy (IRb) to avoid reweighting; all sequences aligned with MAFFT v7.
- Maximum Likelihood (ML) phylogenetic analysis using RAxML v.8.1.17 under the GTRGAMMA model, with 1,000 bootstrap replicates for nodal support.
- Bayesian Inference (BI) using MrBayes v.3.2.4 under the GTR+I+G model; MCMC run for 12 million generations, repeated twice, trees sampled every 100 generations, first 25% discarded as burn-in.
- Maximum Parsimony (MP) analysis in MEGA X, with a bootstrap consensus tree from 1,000 replicates (MP consensus tree from the 5 most parsimonious trees, length = 1883, consistency index = 0.77, retention index = 0.90).
