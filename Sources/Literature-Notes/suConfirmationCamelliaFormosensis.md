# The confirmation of Camellia formosensis (Theaceae) as an independent species based on DNA sequence analyses

> **Authors:** Mong-Huai Su, Chang-Fu Hsieh, Chih-Hua Tsou
> **Year:** 2009
> **Journal:** 
> **DOI:** 
> [Open in Zotero](zotero://select/library/items/H47YZ7I8)

## Abstract

The endemic ‘Taiwanese wild tea’ represents an invaluable natural resource for the tea industry. The taxon is distributed in the central, southern and eastern regions of Taiwan with populations shrinking dramatically in the past decade. Taxonomically, it has long been treated as a lower taxon either under Camellia sinensis var. sinensis or C. sinensis var. assamica. Nevertheless, our recent morphological study indicated that this taxon is different from the other two taxa. In this report, we provide molecular evidence for reassessing its systematic status. Samples representing all the wild populations that are available in Taiwan were sequenced along with samples from the other two taxa at the introns 12-16 and 23 of the RPB2 gene of nuclear DNA. Phylogenetic analysis recovered a monophyletic group of ‘Taiwanese wild tea’ well separated from the other taxa. In addition, genetic distances and previous phenetic analyses all supported the identity of this species. A new combination of C. formosensis (Masamune et Suzuki) M. H. Su, C. F. Hsieh et C. H. Tsou is proposed herein.

## Key Claims Relevant to Tea KG

- Proposes a new taxonomic combination: **Camellia formosensis (Masamune et Suzuki) M. H. Su, C. F. Hsieh et C. H. Tsou comb. nov.** (Chinese: 台灣山茶), elevating "Taiwanese wild tea" from a lower taxon (variety/forma of C. sinensis) to an independent species.
- Basionym is *Thea formosensis* Masamune et Suzuki (Suzuki, 1937); a neotype is designated: Formosa (Taiwan), Taityu (Nantou Co.), Gyoti (Yuchih), 8 Nov 1935, S. Suzuki s.n. (TAI no. 218078).
- *Camellia sinensis* (L.) O. Kuntze forma *formosensis* Kitamura (1950) and *C. sinensis* subsp. *buisanensis* (Sasaki) Lu & Yang (1987) are treated as synonyms of the basionym; Ming (2000) had merged the taxon into *C. sinensis* var. *sinensis*.
- Molecular evidence used: nuclear DNA sequences of RPB2 gene introns 12-16 and intron 23 across 35 samples (13 C. formosensis, 8 C. sinensis var. sinensis, 11 C. sinensis var. assamica, plus 3 outgroup species).
- Phylogenetic analysis (MP and NJ) recovered all 13 C. formosensis samples as a single monophyletic clade with high bootstrap support (92% MP / 99% NJ), while the 19 combined samples of C. sinensis var. sinensis and var. assamica were polytomous/unresolved (interpreted as due to a long history of cultivation and hybridization/introgression between those two taxa).
- Average pairwise genetic distance (uncorrected "p"): C. formosensis vs. C. sinensis var. assamica = 0.0092±0.0022; C. formosensis vs. C. sinensis var. sinensis = 0.0079±0.0026; C. sinensis var. sinensis vs. var. assamica = 0.0043±0.0023. The two C. formosensis-to-cultivar distances are roughly double the sinensis-assamica distance and are described as equivalent to average inter-species distances within section Thea (cf. Xiao 2001/Xiao and Parks 2003: 0.0087±0.0054).
- Within C. formosensis, three geographically defined populations were resolved into two subclades: a "South population" (southern Taiwan) and a combined "Nantou population" + "East population" (Taitung) subclade. Average genetic distances among these populations: Nantou–South 0.0038±0.0002, East–South 0.0033±0.0002, Nantou–East 0.0016±0.0006 — all smaller than the sinensis/assamica interspecific distance (0.0043).
- Morphological/phenetic support cited: an earlier study (Su et al., 2007) found 52 C. formosensis specimens formed a distinct clade separated from 113 specimens of the other two taxa, distinguished by glabrous ovaries and winter buds; the East population's hairy leaves/buds are interpreted as a local adaptation to windy winter monsoon conditions rather than evidence against monophyly.
- Independent corroboration cited: Vijayan et al. (2009, in press at time of writing) found a similar species-level separation using nrITS sequences.
- A black tea cultivar, **TTES No. 18**, was developed by hybridizing Taiwanese wild tea (C. formosensis, as the paternal/pollen parent) with a cultivar from Burma (as the maternal parent) — a concrete cross/pedigree relationship.
- C. formosensis is endemic to Taiwan, distributed in central, southern, and southeastern Taiwan (Nantou, Yunlin, Chiayi, Kaohsiung, Pingtung, and Taitung Counties) at elevations of 800–1,800 m; wild populations have been declining sharply.

## Methods Used

- DNA extraction from silica-gel-dried leaf tissue, following Vijayan and Tsou (2008) protocol.
- PCR amplification and sequencing of two nuclear DNA regions: RPB2 gene introns 12-16 and intron 23 (RPB2 is a single-copy gene encoding the second-largest subunit of RNA polymerase II); primers listed (some from Xiao 2001, some newly designed for this study).
- Sample set: 35 total samples — 13 C. formosensis (11 wild-collected, 2 from tea germplasm garden), 8 C. sinensis var. sinensis, 11 C. sinensis var. assamica, and 3 outgroup species (C. furfuracea, C. transarisanensis, C. brevistyla); voucher specimens deposited at TAI/HAST herbaria; sequences deposited in GenBank (accession nos. listed per sample).
- Sequence alignment with CLUSTAL_X v1.83 (Thompson et al., 1997).
- Phylogenetic analysis via maximum parsimony (MP, branch-and-bound search, strict consensus tree) and neighbor joining (NJ), both performed in PAUP v0.4b10 (Swofford, 2001).
- Branch support assessed via bootstrapping with 1000 replicates.
- Genetic distances calculated using the uncorrected ("p") distance index under PAUP's distance criterion.
- Comparison against prior morphological/numerical taxonomy (Su et al., 2007, based on 52 C. formosensis and 113 other-taxa specimens) and prior molecular studies (Lai et al., 2001, RAPD/ISSR; Wachira et al., 2001, RAPD/AFLP; Xiao, 2001 and Xiao and Parks, 2003, RPB2; Vijayan et al., 2009, nrITS).

## Conflicts With Other Sources

- **This paper's proposed species elevation supersedes the taxonomic treatment used in [[laiAssessmentGeneticRelationships]] (Lai et al. 2001), which is itself cited here as prior molecular work being reassessed.** Lai et al. (2001) treated Taiwanese wild tea as *C. sinensis var. assamica f. formosensis* Kitam. (a form under the assamica variety), consistent with the RPB2-based finding here that (pre-reclassification) *C. sinensis* var. sinensis and var. assamica samples were themselves polytomous/unresolved due to cultivation history — but this paper's monophyletic, well-separated *C. formosensis* clade (92%/99% bootstrap) is the basis for elevating the taxon to full species rank, which Lai et al.'s treatment predates.
- **TTES No. 18 parentage corroborated by [[TTESNo18]]**, the official TBRS cultivar page, which agrees Burma is the maternal parent and Taiwanese wild tea the paternal parent, and additionally names the specific accession codes (B-729 maternal, B-607 paternal) not given in this paper.
