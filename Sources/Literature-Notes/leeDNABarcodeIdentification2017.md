# DNA barcode and identification of the varieties and provenances of Taiwan's domestic and imported made teas using ribosomal internal transcribed spacer 2 sequences

> **Authors:** Shih-Chieh Lee, Chia-Hsiang Wang, Cheng-En Yen, Chieh Chang
> **Year:** 2017
> **Journal:** Journal of Food and Drug Analysis
> **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jfda.2016.06.008
> [Open in Zotero](zotero://select/library/items/PXTBQ8JS)

## Abstract

The major aim of made tea identiﬁcation is to identify the variety and provenance of the tea plant. The present experiment used 113 tea plants [Camellia sinensis (L.) O. Kuntze] housed at the Tea Research and Extension Substation, from which 113 internal transcribed spacer 2 (ITS2) fragments, 104 trnL intron, and 98 trnL-trnF intergenic sequence region DNA sequences were successfully sequenced. The similarity of the ITS2 nucleotide sequences between tea plants housed at the Tea Research and Extension Substation was 0.379e0.994. In this polymerase chain reaction-ampliﬁed noncoding region, no varieties possessed identical sequences. Compared with the trnL intron and trnL-trnF intergenic sequence fragments of chloroplast cpDNA, the proportion of ITS2 nucleotide sequence variation was large and is more suitable for establishing a DNA barcode database to identify tea plant varieties. After establishing the database, 30 imported teas and 35 domestic made teas were used in this model system to explore the feasibility of using ITS2 sequences to identify the varieties and provenances of made teas. A phylogenetic tree was constructed using ITS2 sequences with the unweighted pair group method with arithmetic mean, which indicated that the same variety of tea plant is likely to be successfully categorized into one cluster, but contamination from other tea plants was also detected. This result provides molecular evidence that the similarity between important tea varieties in Taiwan remains high. We suggest a direct, wide collection of made tea and original samples of tea plants to establish an ITS2 sequence molecular barcode identiﬁcation database to identify the varieties and provenances of tea plants. The DNA barcode comparison method can satisfy the need for a rapid, low-cost, frontline differentiation of the large amount of made teas from Taiwan and abroad, and can provide molecular evidence of their varieties and provenances.

## Key Claims Relevant to Tea KG

- Sampled 113 tea plant varieties [*Camellia sinensis* (L.) O. Kuntze] housed at the Tea Research and Extension Substation (TRES), Taitung Substation: 20 TRES breeding varieties (*C. sinensis*), 72 small-leaf varieties (*C. sinensis* var. *sinensis*), 9 large-leaf varieties (*C. sinensis* var. *assamica*), and 12 Taiwanese native wild tea plants (*C. sinensis*).
- Successfully sequenced 113 ITS2 (internal transcribed spacer 2) fragments, 104 chloroplast trnL intron sequences, and 98 trnL-trnF intergenic spacer (IGS) sequences.
- ITS2 sequence similarity among the 113 TRES varieties ranged 0.379–0.994, with 149 variant sites out of 169 aligned sites (88.1%); no two varieties shared an identical ITS2 sequence.
- ITS2 length ranged 149–164 bp (average 159.3 bp): a "long group" of 160(±1) bp (101 varieties) and a "short group" of 150(±1) bp (7 varieties); longest was TTES No.5 (164 bp), shortest were No.88 Kyang and No.114 Dah Pan (149 bp); 5 varieties (Nos. 4, 5, 30, 59, 89) fit neither group.
- Highest ITS2 similarity among all 113 varieties was between TTES No.14 and Dah Terng; largest difference was between Woan Joong and Hwang Gan.
- Chloroplast trnL intron (104 varieties, 510–515 bp) similarity ranged 0.948–1.000; trnL-trnF IGS (98 varieties, 387–394 bp) similarity ranged 0.979–1.000 — both cpDNA markers were much more conserved than ITS2 and judged insufficient for variety-level resolution, so ITS2 was selected as the primary barcode marker.
- Among 25 "important and distinctive" widely cultivated Taiwan varieties, ITS2 similarity ranged from 0.529 (Yabukita vs. TTES No.19) to 0.984 (TTES No.18 vs. TTES No.8).
- Phylogenetic trees (UPGMA, NJ, ME, MP, Maximum Likelihood; bootstrap = 500) gave concordant topologies. No.76 Hwang Gan was the most divergent/independent variety (grouped with No.71 Niou Shyy Wu at only 0.531 similarity).
- No.85 Chyh Ya Sun Cha, No.93 Lai Tou, and No.100 Shuei Jing consistently clustered together across NJ/ME/MP methods, matching prior leaf/bud morphology classification (Wang & Ho 1997) and ISSR marker classification (Cai & Lin 2007).
- A separate Taiwanese wild *Camellia* cluster (No.84 Sun Cha, No.96 Fong Huang, No.98 Mei Yuan, No.99 Min Ghai, No.102 Yung Kang) was identified; authors conclude Taiwanese wild *Camellia* contributes a high proportion of the genetic variation seen among Taiwan tea varieties.
- UPGMA tree: nearly all 113 varieties/made-tea samples fell into one cluster at genetic distance ~0.055 (except foreign sample TJK90 from Mae Salong, Thailand); a genetic distance of ~0.009 was identified as a useful boundary for separating made-tea samples into variety-level clusters (e.g., 11 made teas clustered as TTES No.12 Chinhsuan; 5 made teas clustered as No.107 Chin Shin Oolong).
- ITS2 phylogenetic trees could reliably identify **variety** but showed **no regular pattern for identifying provenance** (domestic PJK- vs. imported TJK- samples) among the 65 tested made-tea samples.
- Of 30 imported made teas, 19 (especially from Vietnam and Indonesia) matched Taiwan cultivars: 12 similar to TRES breeding varieties, 10 similar to TTES No.12, 2 similar to TTES No.13, plus 5 identified as Chin Shin Oolong and 2 as Shy Jih Chuen — interpreted as evidence that Taiwan-bred cultivars (esp. TTES No.12/13) have been moved abroad through unofficial channels.
- For TTES No.12 (Chinhsuan) made-tea samples from 7 Taiwan production areas plus the TRES reference plant (19 samples total): ITS2 similarity ranged 0.910–1.000; every made-tea sample differed from the TRES reference sequence.
- Region-level SNP frequency (of 160 aligned ITS2 sites) for Chinhsuan: lowest in Jhushan, Nantou at 1.3% (2/160). The text calls Pinglin, New Taipei the highest at 9.4% (15/160), but Table 4 gives Yilan 12.5% (20/160) — see Conflicts; the three Hualien samples (PJK1034, 1036, 1038) were identical to one another.
- Average SNP frequency: domestic Chinhsuan 5.1% (8.1/160); domestic Chin Shin Oolong 8.8% (14.1/160); imported/foreign Chinhsuan 11.6% (18.5/160) — roughly double the domestic Chinhsuan rate and higher than domestic Chin Shin Oolong.
- Highest single-site-group SNP frequency measured: Chin Shin Oolong from Taitung (PJK452–454), 14.4% (23/160 sites).
- Across the three focal varieties: No.13 Tzuiyu had 14 SNP sites (8.8% of total sites); No.107 Chin Shin Oolong had 53 SNPs (33.1%, the most of the three); No.12 Chinhsuan had 32 SNPs (20.0%).
- 42 SNPs (across 13 made-tea samples) were identified as production-area-characteristic/diagnostic sites (e.g., TRES Chinhsuan reference: c.112G>A; TJK96 from Da Lat, Vietnam: c.3G>C; TJK90 from Mae Salong, Thailand carried 11 area-characteristic SNPs, the most of any sample) — but when the full sample set was analyzed together, 9 of these 13 samples lost their distinguishing SNPs, showing provenance-diagnostic SNPs are context/dataset-dependent and not fully robust.
- Overall conclusion: ITS2 DNA barcoding (with SNP-level refinement) is effective for **variety/cultivar identification** of made teas but not reliable alone for **provenance identification**; genetic similarity among important Taiwan tea varieties remains high, and cross-border movement of Taiwan-bred varieties (esp. TTES No.12/13) appears to explain some of the imported-tea similarity to Taiwan cultivars.
- Background claims (from cited literature, stated in Introduction): tea originated in Yunnan Province, southwest China, and comprises five subspecies and two varieties; only *C. sinensis* (L.) O. Kuntze and its cultivars are widely cultivated today. Three source types are recognized worldwide: *C. sinensis* L. (small leaf, "China" type), *C. assamica* Masters (big leaf, "Assam" type; = *C. sinensis* var. *assamica*), and *C. assamica* ssp. *lasiocalyx* (intermediate leaf, "Cambod" type).
- Taiwan's commercial tea varieties are described as: a small-leaf variety historically imported from mainland China (used for green tea and some fermented teas), a large-leaf variety imported from India (used for black tea), and new large/medium/small-leaf varieties bred by TRES via crossbreeding and seed selection between imported varieties and native Taiwanese large-leaf wild tea (*C. sinensis*).
- The BEL-1/BEL-3 ITS2 primer pair (Chiou et al., 2007) was originally validated across 120 species / 52 families and successfully amplified ITS2 in 300 medicinal herbs; here it successfully amplified ITS2 in all tested tea samples.

## Methods Used

- Plant material: apical bud + two youngest leaves from a single strain of each of 113 tea plant varieties housed at TRES Taitung Substation, fixed/dried/processed (unfermented, green-tea style) for analysis (collected March 2008).
- Additional made-tea samples: 35 domestic Taiwanese made teas (code prefix PJK) and 30 imported made teas (code prefix TJK), sourced via the Tungding Substation of TRES (collected November 2011).
- Genomic DNA extraction via CTAB (cetyl trimethyl ammonium bromide) method with modifications (liquid-nitrogen grinding, chloroform:isoamyl alcohol extraction, sodium acetate/ethanol precipitation).
- PCR amplification of three noncoding DNA barcode marker regions:
  - Ribosomal ITS2 (internal transcribed spacer 2), using degenerate mixed primer pair BEL-1/BEL-3 (Chiou et al., 2007).
  - Chloroplast trnL intron, using universal primers c/d (Taberlet et al., 1991).
  - Chloroplast trnL-trnF intergenic spacer (IGS), using universal primers e/f (Taberlet et al., 1991).
- Agarose gel electrophoresis (1.6%) to verify PCR products.
- Sanger sequencing on an ABI PRISM 377 DNA sequencer; bidirectional sequencing with each PCR product sequenced at least 3 times from both 5' and 3' ends to confirm sequence accuracy.
- Sequence compilation/alignment with BioEdit v7.0; sequence comparison via BLAST against NCBI GenBank and an in-house sequence database.
- Sequence similarity calculation and multiple sequence alignment using BioEdit and MEGA 6.
- Phylogenetic tree construction from ITS2 alignments using five clustering/tree methods: UPGMA (primary), neighbor-joining (NJ), minimum evolution (ME), maximum parsimony (MP), and maximum likelihood; bootstrap support assessed with 500 replicates.
- SNP-level analysis of ITS2 sequences comparing made-tea samples of identical variety but different geographic production origin, to identify production-area-characteristic/diagnostic SNP sites.
- Sample sizes: 113 ITS2 sequences, 104 trnL intron sequences, 98 trnL-trnF IGS sequences from TRES reference plants; 65 made-tea samples (30 imported + 35 domestic) used for variety/provenance identification testing.
