## Abstract

*Note: This is a Brief Communication with no formal abstract section in the published article (it opens directly with body text after the keywords list). The paragraph below is a faithful one-paragraph summary drawn only from the paper's content.*

The authors developed a genome-wide 200K Affymetrix Axiom SNP array for tea (Camellia sinensis) by selecting 179,970 SNPs (from an initial pool of 5,360,472 SNPs with minor allele frequency >0.1, drawn from 218.9 million high-quality variants identified in prior chromosome-level reference genome and re-sequencing work) that were uniformly distributed across the genome and located on or near genes. The array achieved an average sample call rate of 98.1% and high reproducibility on duplicated samples ('LJ43' 99.71%, 'BHZ' 99.67%), with genotype consistency against re-sequencing data of 21 overlapped cultivars averaging 84.07% (range 64.14–91.93%). Using the array to genotype an F1 mapping population of 327 individuals, the authors merged 18,226 polymorphic SNPs into an existing 2b-RAD genetic linkage map, yielding a high-density map of 5325 bin markers across 15 linkage groups spanning 2107.01 cM, which showed strong collinearity with published CSS ('SCZ', 'LJ43') and ancient tea ('DASZ') genomes. Using this map, they identified four seed-setting-rate (SSR) QTLs (LG1, 2, 5, 15) and, within the LG15 QTL interval, validated a candidate apyrase gene (TEA033353.1) whose ectopic expression in Arabidopsis delayed bolting/flowering, indicating a negative role in fertility. Finally, population structure and phylogenetic analysis of 142 Chinese tea cultivars distinguished Camellia sinensis var. assamica (CSA), Camellia sinensis var. sinensis (CSS), and an artificial-hybrid transitional type, and a genome-wide FST scan between CSS and other cultivars identified selective-sweep regions (1.24% of windows with FST > 0.15, concentrated in LG1, 9, 13) including an isoeugenol synthase gene cluster (LG1, green tea aroma biosynthesis) and a BRI1-like 2 gene (TEA021769, LG9, >10-fold higher expression in CSA than CSS), demonstrating the array's utility for origin analysis and forward genetics in tea.

## Key Claims Relevant to Tea KG

- Cultivated tea comprises two morphologically distinct varieties: Camellia sinensis var. assamica (CSA) — large leaves, cold-sensitive, arborous/semi-arborous habit — and Camellia sinensis var. sinensis (CSS) — smaller leaves, cold-tolerant, shrub/semi-shrub habit.
- 218.9 million high-quality variants were previously identified from chromosome-level reference genomes and re-sequencing data of tea plants (citing Wang et al. 2020); 5,360,472 SNPs with minor allele frequency (MAF) >0.1 were selected from this set, and 179,970 SNPs (uniformly distributed genome-wide, on/near genes) were used to build the 200K Affymetrix Axiom SNP array (data deposited at NCBI GEO accession GSE182082).
- Array quality: average sample call rate 98.1%; technical reproducibility on duplicate samples was 99.71% for cultivar 'LJ43' and 99.67% for cultivar 'BHZ'.
- Genotype consistency between the SNP array and re-sequencing data across 21 overlapping tea cultivars ranged 64.14%–91.93% (average 84.07%); 78.3% of discrepant genotypes were heterozygous-by-resequencing but homozygous-by-array. Sanger sequencing of 18 discrepant SNPs confirmed 8 matched the array and 10 matched re-sequencing, indicating neither method is 100% accurate.
- A high-density genetic linkage map was built from an F1 population of 327 individuals (327 progeny), genotyped with the 200K array; 18,226 polymorphic SNPs were merged with an existing 2b-RAD linkage map (Xu et al. 2018), giving ~3-fold more markers. After binning, the map has 5325 bin markers across 15 linkage groups (LGs) spanning 2107.01 cM total, with bin marker density ranging from 0.31 cM/marker (LG3, densest) to 0.52 cM/marker (LG12, sparsest), average 0.39 cM.
- Chromosomal collinearity analysis showed the new genetic map is more collinear with CSS reference genomes ('SCZ' and 'LJ43') than with the ancient tea genome ('DASZ'), implying substantial genomic differences between CSS and ancient tea accessions.
- QTL mapping for seed setting rate (SSR), using phenotypes from the F1 population grown at Hangzhou (2020) and Shengzhou (2019–2020), China, identified four overlapping QTLs on LG1, LG2, LG5, and LG15.
- The LG15 SSR QTL was fine-mapped to a 752.9 kb interval on the 'SCZ' reference genome (Xia et al. 2020), containing 16 candidate genes, including an apyrase gene (TEA033353.1) highly expressed in flower and fruit tissue.
- Functional validation: overexpressing TEA033353.1 (under CaMV 35S promoter) in transgenic Arabidopsis produced late bolting/flowering phenotypes (two independent lines, OX1/OX2), indicating this apyrase gene negatively regulates fertility — homologous to Arabidopsis AtAPY6/AtAPY7, known fertility-associated apyrases.
- Population structure and phylogenetic analysis of 142 tea cultivars bred from different provinces of China: at K=3, CSA, CSS, and a "transitional type" were clearly distinguished; many transitional-type cultivars are artificial hybrids between CSA and CSS parentage.
- CSS cultivars clustered tightly in the phylogenetic tree, indicating substantially lower within-group genetic variation for CSS compared to the remaining (CSA/transitional) cultivars.
- Genome-wide Wright's FST scan comparing CSS vs. remaining cultivars found 1.24% of FST windows under strong selective sweep (FST > 0.15), with the largest-effect regions on LG1, LG9, and LG13.
- Two candidate selection signatures identified in CSS: (1) an isoeugenol synthase gene cluster on LG1, linked to biosynthesis of (E)-isoeugenol, a characteristic aroma compound of green tea; (2) a BRI1-like 2 gene (TEA021769) on LG9, whose expression in CSA is >10-fold higher than in CSS (BRI1 is a brassinosteroid receptor kinase controlling plant development).

## Methods Used

- Genome-wide SNP array design: SNP discovery from 218.9 million variants (chromosome-level reference genomes + re-sequencing data), MAF filtering (>0.1) to 5,360,472 SNPs, then selection of 179,970 uniformly-distributed, gene-proximal SNPs for a 200K Affymetrix Axiom SNP array (data at NCBI GEO GSE182082).
- SNP array quality assessment: sample call rate calculation, technical-replicate reproducibility testing (duplicate samples), cross-platform validation against whole-genome re-sequencing data (21 overlapping cultivars).
- Sanger sequencing PCR verification of discordant SNP genotypes (18 discrepant SNPs).
- High-density genetic linkage map construction: SNP-array genotyping of an F1 mapping population (n=327), integration/merging with a pre-existing 2b-RAD sequencing-based linkage map, marker binning, construction of 15 linkage groups.
- Chromosomal collinearity/synteny analysis between the genetic map and three published tea reference genomes ('SCZ', 'LJ43', 'DASZ').
- QTL mapping for a quantitative trait (seed setting rate, SSR) using multi-year, multi-site phenotypic data (Hangzhou 2020; Shengzhou 2019–2020).
- Candidate gene identification within QTL intervals and tissue-specific gene expression profiling (16 candidate genes, RNA expression heatmap).
- Transgenic functional validation: overexpression of candidate gene TEA033353.1 under the CaMV 35S promoter in Arabidopsis thaliana, phenotypic scoring of transgenic lines (bolting/flowering timing).
- Population structure analysis (K-value clustering, K=3) of 142 tea cultivars.
- Phylogenetic tree construction from SNP genotype data of the 142 cultivars.
- Wright's F-statistic (FST) genome scan (windowed FST analysis) between CSS and remaining cultivars to detect selective sweep regions (threshold FST > 0.15).

## Conflicts With Other Sources

- **Taxonomic rank of "assamica" differs from [[singhFirstgenerationHaplotypeMap2022]].** This paper treats CSA as *Camellia sinensis var. assamica* — a variety within *C. sinensis* — consistent with the Taiwan-focused papers in this vault (Hu 2014/2023, Lee 2017, Su et al.). Singh et al. (2022) instead treat the Assam type as its own species, *Camellia assamica*. Same underlying germplasm group, different taxonomic rank.
