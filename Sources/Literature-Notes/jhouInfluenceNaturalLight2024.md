# Influence of natural light intensities on growth and content of catechins and caffeine in Camellia formosensis leaves and tea infusion

> **Authors:** Hong-Chyi Jhou, Chung-I Chen, Jia-Yi Shen, Chieh-Ting Wang, Fang-Chih Chang
> **Year:** 2024
> **Journal:** BioResources
> **DOI:** 10.15376/biores.20.1.877-887
> [Open in Zotero](zotero://select/library/items/ZUMDL5P2)

## Abstract

Camellia formosensis is a significant under the forest canopy crop in Taiwan. This study investigated the composition and yield variations of native Camellia formosensis tea leaves across different relative light intensities levels and seasons. Increasing the relative intensity of natural light resulted in a corresponding increase in the dry weight of C. formosensis leaves. At a relative light intensity of 70%, the dry weight of the leaves per unit area was 15.8 g. However, the yield of C. formosensis was lower at relative light intensities of 40% or 20%. A positive correlation was observed between relative light intensities and the contents of catechin gallate (CG), epicatechin gallate (ECG), and gallocatechin gallate (GCG) and relative light intensity. At 20% relative light intensity, the mean CG, ECG, and GCG contents were 4.97, 0.57, and 4.43 mg/g, respectively. These values surged to 6.93, 2.23, and 7.53 mg/g, respectively, at 70% relative light intensity. Caffeine content exhibited an inverse relationship with relative light intensity, declining notably after surpassing 70%. Moreover, C. formosensis leaves harvested in summer under 70% relative light intensity boasted higher contents of CG, epicatechin (EC), ECG, epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG), and caffeine than those harvested in spring. Ester catechin content surpassed non-ester catechins, with the former consistently higher at 100% relative light intensity, irrespective of the season.

## Key Claims Relevant to Tea KG

- *Camellia formosensis* is a member of Theaceae, genus *Camellia*, native to mid- to low-altitude mountains of Taiwan (700–1650 m); an evergreen small tree reaching up to 8 m in height with trunk diameter up to 40 cm.
- *C. formosensis* was previously treated as a variety of *Camellia sinensis* (L.) Kuntze but was confirmed as an independent, Taiwan-endemic species based on nucleic acid (DNA) sequence analysis (Su et al. 2009).
- In 2017 the Red List of Vascular Plants of Taiwan listed *C. formosensis* as Near Threatened.
- *C. formosensis* tea was first documented in the Zhuluo County Memorial (1717), noted for a unique taste and green color resembling *Usnea*; harvesting was historically limited by dangerous roads and Aboriginal-territory access.
- During Japanese colonial rule, *C. formosensis* was cultivated for black tea production, and interest in it continued after the colonial period.
- In 1999, Taiwan Tea No. 18 (Red Jade) was bred by crossbreeding *C. formosensis* with Myanmar large-leaf Burma (B-729), using B-729 as the female parent; Red Jade is noted for ease of cultivation and distinct mint/cinnamon flavor undertones, demonstrating *C. formosensis*'s value as a breeding parent.
- The Tea and Beverage Research Station identified a *C. formosensis* source from Yongkang Mountain, Taitung County, and in 2019 officially released it as a fast-growing, disease- and pest-resistant cultivar named Yongkang tea (Taiwan Tea No. 24), noted for a unique flavor profile.
- Other commercial *C. formosensis* products cited: Liouguei tea and Yuchi Farmers Association black tea.
- Experimental design: wild *C. formosensis* trees at three sites (Tiandi, Pingxihu, Dalun) in Qingshuigou Tract, National Taiwan University Experimental Forest (800–1100 m altitude), subjected to four relative light intensities (RLI): 100% (control, unshaded), 70%, 40%, 20%, with 3 replicate plants per treatment.
- Leaf yield (bud-leaf count per unit area) increased with increasing RLI: highest mean bud-leaf count (69) at 100% RLI, lowest (13) at 20% RLI (differences not statistically significant between levels).
- Dry weight of leaves per unit area was highest at 70% RLI (15.8 g); yield (both bud count and dry weight) was markedly lower at 40% and especially 20% RLI, and dry weight actually declined somewhat at 100% RLI relative to 70%.
- At 20% or 40% RLI, *C. formosensis* budded only in March–April with no subsequent flush; at 70% or 100% RLI, budding also occurred in summer and autumn. No budding occurred in winter (attributed to low rainfall, <50 mm/month from Oct 2023, and declining temperatures, mean 21.2 °C in Oct 2023).
- Catechin gallate (CG), epicatechin gallate (ECG), and gallocatechin gallate (GCG) contents correlated positively with RLI: at 20% RLI mean values were CG 4.97 mg/g, ECG 0.57 mg/g, GCG 4.43 mg/g; at 70% RLI these rose to CG 6.93 mg/g, ECG 2.23 mg/g, GCG 7.53 mg/g. CG, ECG, and GCG then decreased again at 100% RLI.
- GC and EGCG contents decreased as RLI decreased (i.e., positively correlated with light), whereas EC content was negatively correlated with RLI (higher at lower light).
- Caffeine content declined with decreasing RLI, with a notable drop after RLI exceeded 70% (i.e., caffeine was lower at 100% than at 70%).
- Season × light interaction (at 70% RLI): summer-harvested leaves had higher mean CG (10.4 mg/g), EC (4.03 mg/g), ECG (2.37 mg/g), EGCG (69.07 mg/g), and caffeine (31.97 mg/g) than spring-harvested leaves (CG 6.93, EC 3.7, ECG 2.23, EGCG 48.3, caffeine 29.4 mg/g).
- Ester catechins (EGCG, ECG, GCG) consistently exceeded nonester catechins (EGC, catechin, EC, GC, CG grouping as used in the paper's ester/nonester split) in content across all seasons and light levels; ester catechin dominance was highest at 100% RLI regardless of season.
- Study conclusions: (1) *C. formosensis* is suitable for understory/under-forest cultivation, but annual yield drops sharply below 40% RLI, making it economically unviable at low light; (2) yield is higher in spring than in summer/autumn, so management (irrigation, care) should be optimized in Jan–Feb to mitigate water shortage; (3) 100% RLI gives the highest yield and catechin content but risks excess bitterness, so understory planting with moderate canopy pruning/thinning is recommended to balance yield and quality; (4) because ester catechin content in *C. formosensis* exceeds nonester catechin content, and ester catechins have strong antioxidant activity, processing *C. formosensis* into tea or medical/health products could enhance its commercial value.
- Full catechin/caffeine dataset (Table 1) reports individual-plant HPLC values (CG, EC, ECG, EGC [mostly ND/not detected], EGCG, GC, GCG, caffeine, mg/g) for each of the four RLI treatments (20/40/70/100%), n=3 plants per treatment.

## Methods Used

- Field/light-treatment design: natural forest-canopy shading used to create four relative light intensity (RLI) treatments — 100% (control/unshaded), 70%, 40%, 20% — with light measured using a PG200N spectral PAR meter (UPRtek, Taiwan); 3 replicate wild *C. formosensis* trees per treatment across 3 sites.
- Seasonal sampling: bud leaves (one-tip-two-leaf new shoots) harvested from a fixed 30 × 30 cm² marked area per tree in April (spring), June (summer), and September (autumn); ambient max light intensity recorded per season (1250, 1765, 1413 µmol m⁻²s⁻¹ respectively).
- Sample preparation: harvested leaves oven-dried at 60 °C (yield samples for 10 h per Fang et al. 2014 method).
- Catechin/caffeine extraction: 0.25 g dried, ground leaf combined with 75% ethanol to 10 mL, extracted 1 h with ultrasonication before and after extraction, then diluted 10× with pure water and filtered.
- Analytical method: high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC, Shimadzu, Japan) with a C6H5 (phenyl) separation column, 10 µL injection volume, flow rate 0.8 mL/min, UV detection at 280 nm, following Song et al. (2012); quantified caffeine and eight catechins (EGCG, ECG, EGC, (+)-catechin, EC, GCG, GC, CG) against Sigma calibration standards.
- Statistical analysis: GraphPad Prism 10 used for data visualization; SPSS version 26 used for analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Bonferroni and Tukey's honest significant difference (HSD) post hoc tests to compare significance among groups (p < 0.05).
