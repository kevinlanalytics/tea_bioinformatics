# SSR-based molecular diagnosis for Taiwan tea cultivars and its application in identifying cultivar composition of the processed tea

> **Authors:** Chih-Yi Hu, Hsien-Tung Tsai, Chui-Feng Chiu, Tsung-Chen Su, Nguyen Hoang Khoi Le, Shu-Dan Yeh
> **Year:** 2023
> **Journal:** Journal of Food and Drug Analysis
> **DOI:** 10.38212/2224-6614.3465
> [Open in Zotero](zotero://select/library/items/RF5XDNEQ)

## Abstract

Taiwan specialty teas are produced with distinct manufacturing processes from speciﬁc cultivars of tea plants in Camellia. Due to the widespread transplantation of Taiwan tea cultivars and active international trading of tea materials, an accurate and reliable method to identify tea cultivars at the border is vital to protect the image of premium Taiwan specialty teas. In this study, we introduced the Taiwan Tea Variety Identiﬁcation (TTVID) kit, a capillary electrophoresis-based multiplex PCR assay consisting of 12 simple sequence repeat (SSR) markers. A database composing these 12 SSR loci genotypes in 144 cultivars was established for marker assessment and molecular diagnosis. The power of discrimination on a locus ranged from 0.7894 to 0.966 and the combined match probability of 12 SSR loci was 5.34e-14. Cultivar pairwise comparison among 144 accessions showed that over 90.6% of the pairs had differential genotypes on at least 10 of 12 SSR loci. Further assessment showed that the TTVID kit could unambiguously recognize the cultivars mixed in the loose-leaf teas processed with various degrees of fermentation and roasting. Our results suggested that this TTVID kit effectively identiﬁed cultivar composition in loose-leaf tea and is helpful for border control in preventing adulteration and fraud in the Taiwan tea market.

## Key Claims Relevant to Tea KG

- Taiwanese tea cultivars are classified into three taxonomies: *Camellia sinensis* var. *sinensis* ("Sinensis"), *C. sinensis* var. *assamica* ("Assamica"), and *Camellia formosensis* ("Formosana"), the last native to Taiwan.
- The authors built the Taiwan Tea Variety Identification (TTVID) kit: a capillary-electrophoresis multiplex-PCR assay of 12 SSR (microsatellite) markers, and genotyped a reference database of 144 Taiwan tea cultivars (115 var. *sinensis*, 17 var. *assamica*, 12 *C. formosensis*).
- The 12 loci sit on 9 of the 15 chromosomes of the *C. sinensis* genome (reference genome: *C. sinensis* var. *sinensis* cultivar 'Tieguanyin', GenBank accession JAFLEL000000000.1).
- 146 total alleles were detected across the 12 loci; allele number per locus ranged from 7 (loci B03 and D01) to 19 (locus C03), average 12.2 alleles/locus.
- Power of discrimination (PD) per locus ranged from 0.7894 (B03) to 0.9666 (B02); combined power of discrimination (CPD) across all 12 loci in the full 144-cultivar panel = 0.99999999999995.
- Combined match probability (CPM) of the 12-locus panel = 5.34e-14; lowest single-locus match probability (PM) = 0.0334 at locus B02, which also has the highest allele count (17) and offers 96.66% confidence to individually assign cultivar identity.
- CPD calculated separately by taxonomic group: Sinensis = 0.9999999999755339987, Assamica = 0.9999999192301602946, Formosana = 0.99999999999907185355 — Assamica had the lowest discriminating power of the three groups.
- Pairwise comparison of all 144 cultivars (10,296 possible pairs): every pair differed at ≥1 of 12 loci; 90.6% of pairs differed at ≥10 of 12 loci; 41.3% of pairs differed at all 12 loci — i.e., the 12-marker panel is sufficient to distinguish essentially all sampled cultivars.
- Genetic-relationship/pedigree claim: cultivar V008 (TTES No. 8) differs from I009 (Jaipuri) at only one of the 12 loci, indicating TTES No. 8 originated from the Jaipuri accession.
- Several Taiwan landrace cultivar pairs show four or fewer differential loci, consistent with landraces having been established from single individuals selected by Taiwan tea growers since 1810.
- TTES No. 12 (V012), an elite cultivar developed before Taiwan's 2004 Plant Variety and Plant Seed Act, has reportedly been transplanted/cultivated in other countries for lower-cost production, then re-imported and mislabeled as premium Taiwan specialty tea — a specific documented case of tea fraud/adulteration motivating this study.
- Cultivars V019 (TTES No. 19), V020 (TTES No. 20), V022 (TTES No. 22), and V025 (TTES No. 25) are legally protected under Taiwan's Plant Variety and Plant Seed Act.
- DNA quality/quantity extracted from processed tea decreases with increasing fermentation (oxidation) and roasting; the C03 locus gave weak chromatographic signal in all processed-tea samples tested, while A02 and D01 gave the strongest signals.
- Applying the TTVID kit to 9 processed Taiwan specialty tea samples (spanning all 8 official specialty-tea manufacturing types): 6 of 9 were identified as single-cultivar products; 3 (ST04, ST05, ST06) showed evidence of multi-cultivar mixtures.
- ST04 (Tongding Oolong) genotyping indicated a mixture of cultivars L001 (Chin-Shin-Oolong) and V012 (TTES No. 12); ST05 (Tieh-Kuan-Yin tea) indicated a mixture dominated by V012 with a secondary component matching or closely related to I031 (Tie-Guan-Yin); ST06 (Black Oolong tea) was predominantly identified as V012 despite degraded signal quality.
- Taiwan imports more than 30,000 metric tons of processed tea annually (average of the preceding five years) from neighboring producers for commodity-tea manufacture, contributing to a negative tea trade balance since 1991.

## Methods Used

- SSR (simple sequence repeat / microsatellite) genotyping via a custom 12-locus capillary-electrophoresis multiplex-PCR assay (the "TTVID kit"), loci grouped into 4 triplex-PCR sets (A, B, C, D of 3 loci each).
- Marker development funnel: 83 SSR primer pairs (drawn from three prior studies) screened on 6 cultivars (4 var. *sinensis*, 2 var. *assamica*) by agarose gel electrophoresis → 20 candidate loci further assessed for allele number/polymorphism information content (PIC) and motif length in 120 Taiwan cultivars → 12 final loci selected for the multiplex kit.
- Multiplex-ready PCR method: forward primers 5'-tagged with a universal sequence (5'ACGACGTTGTAAA3') and labeled with one of three fluorescent dyes (5-FAM, JOE, TAMRA/ROX in initial screening; final kit used 5-FAM/JOE); reverse primers 5'-tagged (5'GTTTAAGTTCCCATTA3') with PIG-tailing (GTTTCTT) to reduce stutter.
- Capillary electrophoresis fragment analysis on a PRISM 3730XL DNA Analyzer (Applied Biosystems), performed by a commercial Genomics company (New Taipei City); allele/fragment sizing with GeneMapper 4.0 software (Applied Biosystems).
- Multiplex PCR carried out with Fast-Run 2x Taq master mix (Protech) for 40 cycles.
- Reference/control samples TTES No. 12 (V012) and Chin-Shin-Oolong (L001) included in every capillary electrophoresis run.
- Genomic DNA extraction from fresh leaves: CTAB method (Doyle & Doyle 1987 protocol); from processed tea samples: Quick-DNA plant/seed miniprep kit (Zymo Research); an alternative kit (Exgene Plant SV, GeneAll) was also trialed but gave lower DNA concentration.
- Plant material: fresh leaves lyophilized and ground with a 2010 Geno/Grinder (SPEX Sample Prep) before storage at −80 °C.
- Population-genetics/forensic statistics: allele frequency, match probability (PM = Σ Gi²), combined match probability (CPM = Π PMi), power of discrimination (PD = 1 − PM), combined power of discrimination (CPD = 1 − Π(1 − PDi)); calculated using FORSTAT and STRAF software.
- Pairwise cultivar-genotype comparison across all 10,296 possible pairs among the 144-cultivar panel, counting number of differential loci per pair.
- Applied validation on 9 real-world Taiwan specialty tea products (covering all 8 official specialty tea manufacturing types, collected Summer 2018–Spring 2021), using two sampling schemes: single-granular sampling (10 individual tea granules genotyped separately per sample) and multi-granular sampling (2 replicate extractions from 10 g of ground tea granules per sample).
- DNA quality/quantity assessment via spectrophotometric 260/280 purity ratio and eluted DNA concentration/yield ratio.
- Genomic location/chromosome assignment of the 12 SSR loci determined against the *C. sinensis* var. *sinensis* cultivar 'Tieguanyin' reference genome (GenBank JAFLEL000000000.1).

## Conflicts With Other Sources

- **TTES No.24 (V024) origin-type labeling conflicts with [[2019TTESNo]], [[jhouInfluenceNaturalLight2024]], and [[TTESNo24]].** This paper's cultivar table groups V024 under "TTES-bred hybrid cultivars" (V-code prefix, labeled "Hybrid" origin) alongside genuine crosses like TTES No.19/20. The TBRS official registration report ([[2019TTESNo]]), the current TBRS cultivar profile page ([[TTESNo24]]), and Jhou et al. (2024) all instead describe TTES No.24 (山蘊/Shan Yun/Yongkang tea) as originating from direct introduction/selection of a single wild *C. formosensis* population from Yongkang Mountain, Taitung — not a controlled hybridization cross. The "Hybrid" label here is likely just this paper's V-code naming convention rather than a factual pedigree claim, but it should not be taken as evidence of a two-parent cross for TTES No.24 in the KG.
